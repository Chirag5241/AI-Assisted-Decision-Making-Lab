We now determine the algorithm $\mathcal{A}$'s optimal curriculum allocation during the training phase ($t = 1, \dots, T$) to minimize expected graduation regret in the multi-action setting ($K \ge 2$).

##### **Training Allocation and Dynamic Evolution:**
During training, $\mathcal{A}$ has a total budget of $T$ feature exposures to distribute among the $n$ features, represented by exposure vector $m = (m_1, \dots, m_n) \in \mathbb{N}^n$ with $\sum_{j=1}^n m_j = T$.

From [[<G> - 1. Multi-Action Reduction]], for each action pair $1 \le k < \ell \le K$, the perceived difference vector evolves deterministically:
$$ p_{k\ell, j}(m_j) = (1 - \psi(m_j)) d_{k\ell, j} + \psi(m_j) p_{k\ell, j}^{(0)} $$
From [[<G> - 2. Multi-Action Test Selection]], when $s \in \mathcal{S}_{k\ell}$, $\mathcal{H}$ selects feature subset:
$$ F_{k\ell}^*(p_{k\ell}(m)) = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} (p_{k\ell}(m))_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} (p_{k\ell}(m))_F $$
From [[<G> - 3. Multi-Action Graduation Regret]], the expected graduation regret is:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(p(m)) = \sum_{1 \le k < \ell \le K} w_{k\ell} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{d_{k\ell}^\top \Sigma d_{k\ell}}{2\pi}} \Big(1 - \rho_{k\ell}\big(F_{k\ell}^*(p_{k\ell}(m)), p_{k\ell}(m)\big)\Big) $$
where $\rho_{k\ell}$ is the Pearson correlation along margin $(k, \ell)$.

##### **The Multi-Margin Bilevel Optimization Problem:**
Minimizing expected graduation regret is equivalent to maximizing the population-weighted correlation. The algorithm $\mathcal{A}$ solves:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\max_{m \in \mathbb{N}^n} \quad & \sum_{1 \le k < \ell \le K} w_{k\ell} \sqrt{\frac{d_{k\ell}^\top \Sigma d_{k\ell}}{2\pi}} \cdot \rho_{k\ell}\Big( F_{k\ell}^*(p_{k\ell}(m)), \; p_{k\ell}(m) \Big) \\
\text{subject to} \quad & \sum_{j=1}^n m_j = T, \quad m_j \in \mathbb{N} \\
& F_{k\ell}^*(p_{k\ell}(m)) = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} (p_{k\ell}(m))_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} (p_{k\ell}(m))_F \quad \forall (k, \ell) \text{ with } w_{k\ell} > 0
\end{aligned}
$$

---

##### **Structure of the Multi-Margin Program:**

1. **Coupled Feature Effects:**  
   In [[4. Curriculum Allocation.md]] ($K = 2$), each exposure $m_j$ affected only one difference vector $p$. Here, assigning an exposure $m_j$ updates coordinate $j$ for **all $\binom{K}{2}$ margins simultaneously**. A feature exposure can simultaneously:
   * Extinguish a distraction along margin $(1, 2)$,
   * Correct a misunderstood feature along margin $(2, 3)$,
   * Reinforce an informative feature along margin $(1, 3)$.

2. **Margin Priority Weighting:**  
   The objective scales each margin by its **effective stakes**:
   $$ \alpha_{k\ell} := w_{k\ell} \sqrt{\frac{d_{k\ell}^\top \Sigma d_{k\ell}}{2\pi}} $$
   Margins with high arrival probability $w_{k\ell}$ and large ground truth variance $d_{k\ell}^\top \Sigma d_{k\ell}$ dominate the objective, while rare margins ($w_{k\ell} \approx 0$) can be safely neglected.

3. **Margin-Specific Dominance and Extinguishment:**  
   For each margin $(k, \ell)$, the perceived salience of feature set $F$ of size $c$ is:
   $$ S_{k\ell}(F; p_{k\ell}(m)) = (p_{k\ell}(m))_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} (p_{k\ell}(m))_F $$
   To ensure target set $F_{k\ell}^*$ is selected by $\mathcal{H}$ at graduation, it must dominate all alternative sets $F' \neq F_{k\ell}^*$:
   $$ S_{k\ell}\big(F_{k\ell}^*; p_{k\ell}(m)\big) > S_{k\ell}\big(F'; p_{k\ell}(m)\big) \quad \forall F' \in \mathcal{F}_c \setminus \{F_{k\ell}^*\} $$
   The minimum cost of extinguishment on margin $(k, \ell)$ is the minimum exposure allocated to $j \notin F_{k\ell}^*$ required to suppress distractor salience below $S_{k\ell}(F_{k\ell}^*)$.

---

##### **Algorithms for Multi-Margin Curriculum Allocation:**

#### Approach A: Dominant-Margin Profile Sieve (Exact for Small Margins)
When the observation space is dominated by a small set of primary margins $\mathcal{M} = \{(k, \ell) : w_{k\ell} > \tau\}$ with $|\mathcal{M}| = M \ll \binom{K}{2}$:
1. Iterate over candidate target profiles $\mathbf{F}^* = (F_{k\ell}^*)_{(k, \ell) \in \mathcal{M}} \in (\mathcal{F}_c)^M$.
2. For each profile, compute the minimal joint exposure vector $m_{\text{ext}} \in \mathbb{N}^n$ satisfying all dominance constraints simultaneously:
   $$ S_{k\ell}\big(F_{k\ell}^*; p(m)\big) > \max_{F' \neq F_{k\ell}^*} S_{k\ell}\big(F'; p(m)\big) \quad \forall (k, \ell) \in \mathcal{M} $$
3. If $\sum_{j=1}^n (m_{\text{ext}})_j \le T$, the profile is feasible. Greedily distribute the remaining surplus budget $T_{\text{rem}} = T - \sum_j (m_{\text{ext}})_j$ to maximize the weighted correlation objective.
4. Return the profile and allocation maximizing the total objective.

#### Approach B: Multi-Margin Greedy Coordinate Allocation (Scalable $O(n T)$)
For larger $K$, we optimize the discrete allocation via greedy coordinate ascent:

```
Algorithm: Multi-Margin Greedy Coordinate Allocation (MM-GCA)
Input: Utilities U, prior beliefs H^(0), budget T, capacity c, weights w_{kl}, residual covariance Gamma
Output: Optimal exposure vector m* in N^n

1. Initialize m = (0, 0, ..., 0)
2. For step = 1 to T:
3.    For each candidate feature j in [n]:
4.       m_cand = m + e_j
5.       Compute updated beliefs:
6.          p_{kl, i}(m_cand) = (1 - psi((m_cand)_i)) d_{kl, i} + psi((m_cand)_i) p_{kl, i}^{(0)}
7.       For each active margin (k, l):
8.          Find F^*_{kl} = argmax_{|F| <= c} (p_{kl}(m_cand))_F^T Gamma_{FF} (p_{kl}(m_cand))_F
9.          Compute correlation rho_{kl}(F^*_{kl}, p_{kl}(m_cand))
10.      Evaluate objective value:
            Obj(j) = sum_{k < l} alpha_{kl} * rho_{kl}(F^*_{kl}, p_{kl}(m_cand))
11.   Select feature with highest marginal gain:
         j* = argmax_{j in [n]} Obj(j)
12.   Update allocation: m_{j*} = m_{j*} + 1
13. Return m* = m
```

*Runtime Analysis:*
* At each round of greedy allocation, there are $n$ candidate features to evaluate.
* For each candidate feature, evaluating $F_{k\ell}^*$ requires searching $\binom{n}{c}$ sets for each of the $M$ active margins. For constant $c$ (e.g. $c \in \{1, 2, 3\}$), this takes $O(M n^c)$ time.
* Evaluating correlation $\rho_{k\ell}$ takes $O(n)$ time.
* Total time across $T$ steps is $O(T M n^{c+1})$, which is polynomial in $n, T$, and easily computes within seconds for practical problem sizes.
