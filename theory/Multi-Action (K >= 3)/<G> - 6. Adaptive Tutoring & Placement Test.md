# <G> 6. Multi-Action Adaptive Tutoring: Placement Testing, Contraction Inference, and Dynamic Graduation

We extend the multi-action framework ($K \ge 3$) to the realistic setting where the algorithm $\mathcal{A}$ **does not have prior knowledge of the student's initial belief matrix $H^{(0)}$ or learning rate $\psi$**, and where the training horizon $T$ is **not fixed ex-ante**, but is chosen adaptively as a stopping time $\tau$.

This note formalizes:
1. **The Diagnostic Placement Test:** Identifying the initial belief geometry $H^{(0)}$ via unassisted multiclass probing ($F = \emptyset$).
2. **Online Inference of Learning Dynamics $\psi$:** Exploiting cross-margin coupling to estimate learner contraction from observed categorical choices.
3. **Adaptive Stopping & Dynamic Graduation ($\tau$):** Formulating the certification stopping time where $\mathcal{A}$ terminates scaffolding once marginal unassisted regret drops below the training cost.

---

### 1. Invariance of Decisions, VoI, and Graduation Regret

Let latent state $x \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma) \in \mathbb{R}^n$, observation $s = Bx \in \mathbb{R}^d$, prior mean $\mu(s) = \Pi x$ with $\Pi = \Sigma B^\top (B \Sigma B^\top)^{-1} B$, and residual covariance $\Gamma = \Sigma - \Pi \Sigma$.

Let $U \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times n}$ be the ground truth utility matrix with rows $u_1, \dots, u_K$, and pairwise differences $d_{k\ell} = u_k - u_\ell$. Let $H \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times n}$ be the human's belief matrix with rows $h_1, \dots, h_K$, and pairwise differences $p_{k\ell} = h_k - h_\ell$.

**Theorem 1 (Scale and Shift Invariance):**
Let $H \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times n}$. Consider any transformed belief matrix $\tilde{H} = \alpha H + \mathbf{1}_K c^\top$ where $\alpha > 0$ and $c \in \mathbb{R}^n$.
Then for all states $x$, observations $s$, and feature subsets $F$:
1. **Action Invariance:** $\arg\max_{k \in [K]} \hat{u}_k(F, x_F, s; \tilde{H}) = \arg\max_{k \in [K]} \hat{u}_k(F, x_F, s; H)$.
2. **VoI Test Selection Invariance:** $F_{\mathcal{H}}^*(s; \tilde{H}) = F_{\mathcal{H}}^*(s; H)$.
3. **Graduation Regret Invariance:** The pairwise Pearson correlation bounds satisfy $\rho_{k\ell}(F, \tilde{p}_{k\ell}) = \rho_{k\ell}(F, p_{k\ell})$ for all $k, \ell \in [K]$.

**Proof:**
1. For any action $k$, the perceived utility under $\tilde{H}$ is:
   $$ \hat{u}_k(F, x_F, s; \tilde{H}) = \sum_{j \in F} (\alpha H_{kj} + c_j) x_j + \sum_{j \notin F} (\alpha H_{kj} + c_j) \mu_j(s) = \alpha \hat{u}_k(F, x_F, s; H) + c^\top \big(E_F^\top x_F + (I - E_F^\top E_F)\mu(s)\big) $$
   For any two actions $k, \ell$, the baseline shift cancels:
   $$ \hat{u}_k(F, x_F, s; \tilde{H}) - \hat{u}_\ell(F, x_F, s; \tilde{H}) = \alpha \big[ \hat{u}_k(F, x_F, s; H) - \hat{u}_\ell(F, x_F, s; H) \big] $$
   Since $\alpha > 0$, the sign of the difference between every pair $(k, \ell)$ is strictly preserved. Hence the $\arg\max$ is identical.
2. By [[<G> - 2. Multi-Action Test Selection]], within any contender cone $\mathcal{S}_{k\ell}$, the test selection is $F^*(s) = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} p_{k\ell, F}^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_{k\ell, F}$. Under $\tilde{H}$, $\tilde{p}_{k\ell} = \alpha p_{k\ell}$, so:
   $$ \arg\max_{|F| \le c} (\tilde{p}_{k\ell})_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} (\tilde{p}_{k\ell})_F = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} \alpha^2 (p_{k\ell})_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} (p_{k\ell})_F = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} (p_{k\ell})_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} (p_{k\ell})_F $$
3. From [[<G> - 3. Multi-Action Graduation Regret]], the correlation is:
   $$ \rho_{k\ell}(F, \alpha p_{k\ell}) = \frac{d_{k\ell}^\top (\Sigma - \Gamma) (\alpha p_{k\ell}) + (\alpha p_{k\ell})_F^\top \Gamma_{F, :} d_{k\ell}}{\sqrt{d_{k\ell}^\top \Sigma d_{k\ell}} \;\; \sqrt{(\alpha p_{k\ell})^\top (\Sigma - \Gamma) (\alpha p_{k\ell}) + (\alpha p_{k\ell})_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} (\alpha p_{k\ell})_F}} $$
   Factoring $\alpha > 0$ from the numerator and $\sqrt{\alpha^2} = \alpha$ from the denominator, $\alpha$ cancels exactly. Thus $\rho_{k\ell}(F, \tilde{p}_{k\ell}) = \rho_{k\ell}(F, p_{k\ell})$. $\blacksquare$

**Significance:** $\mathcal{A}$ does not need to learn the absolute entries of $H^{(0)}$. It only needs to learn the **direction** of each pairwise difference vector:
$$ \bar{p}_{k\ell}^{(0)} := \frac{p_{k\ell}^{(0)}}{\|p_{k\ell}^{(0)}\|} \in \mathbb{S}^{n-1} $$

### 2. The Multiclass Diagnostic Placement Test ($F = \emptyset$)

During the placement phase ($t = 1, \dots, T_{\text{diag}}$), $\mathcal{A}$ reveals no hints ($F_t = \emptyset$).
For each diagnostic case, nature draws $x^{(t)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma)$, and the student observes only $s^{(t)} = Bx^{(t)}$.
The student predicts:
$$ \hat{y}_t = \arg\max_{k \in [K]} h_k^{(0)\top} \mu(s^{(t)}) $$

#### Polyhedral Slicing Identification
Each observed decision $\hat{y}_t = k^*$ implies $K-1$ linear inequalities on the human's belief matrix:
$$ (h_{k^*}^{(0)} - h_\ell^{(0)})^\top \mu(s^{(t)}) \ge 0 \quad \forall \ell \in [K] \setminus \{k^*\} \iff p_{k^*\ell}^{(0)\top} \mu(s^{(t)}) \ge 0 $$

The space of candidate belief matrices compatible with $T_{\text{diag}}$ diagnostic observations is the convex polyhedral cone:
$$ \mathcal{H}_{\text{consistent}} = \left\{ H \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times n} : (h_{\hat{y}_t} - h_\ell)^\top \mu(s^{(t)}) \ge 0 \quad \forall t \in [T_{\text{diag}}], \; \ell \in [K] \right\} $$

#### Estimation Objective
To account for bounded human response noise (temperature $\beta > 0$), $\mathcal{A}$ recovers $\hat{H}^{(0)}$ by minimizing the multiclass margin loss or maximizing the multinomial logit likelihood over normalized rows ($\sum_k h_k = 0, \sum_k \|h_k\|^2 = 1$):
$$ \hat{H}^{(0)} = \arg\min_{H} \sum_{t=1}^{T_{\text{diag}}} \max_{\ell \in [K]} \Big( 0, \; 1 + (h_\ell - h_{\hat{y}_t})^\top \mu(s^{(t)}) \Big) + \frac{\eta}{2} \|H\|_F^2 $$

**Sample Complexity:** By multiclass linear classification sample complexity (Daniely et al., 2015), observing:
$$ T_{\text{diag}} = \mathcal{O}\left( \frac{K \cdot n}{\epsilon^2} \right) $$
unassisted diagnostic items guarantees that with probability $1 - \delta$, the angular reconstruction error $\max_{k,\ell} \|\hat{\bar{p}}_{k\ell}^{(0)} - \bar{p}_{k\ell}^{(0)}\| \le \epsilon$.

### 3. Online Inference of $\psi$ via Cross-Margin Coupling

Once lessons begin ($t > T_{\text{diag}}$), $\mathcal{A}$ selects hint subsets $F_t \subseteq [n]$ with $|F_t| \le c$.
By [[<G> - 1. Multi-Action Reduction]], belief contraction operates identically across all rows:
$$ H_{kj}^{(t+1)} - U_{kj} = \psi(m_j(t)) \big[ H_{kj}^{(0)} - U_{kj} \big] \quad \forall k \in [K], \; j \in F_t $$
Subtracting row $\ell$ from row $k$, the perceived difference vector contracts simultaneously across all $\binom{K}{2}$ margins:
$$ p_{k\ell, j}(m_j) = (1 - \psi(m_j)) d_{k\ell, j} + \psi(m_j) p_{k\ell, j}^{(0)} $$

Let $\psi(m) = \gamma^m$, where $\gamma \in (0, 1)$ is the student's unknown retention parameter.

#### Cross-Margin Coupling as an Estimation Engine
In the binary setting ($K=2$), a hint only provides information along one margin. In the multi-action setting ($K \ge 3$):
1. Exposing feature $j$ contracts beliefs across **all action pairs $(k, \ell)$ where $d_{k\ell, j} \ne 0$**.
2. Within the active-contender cone $\mathcal{S}_{k\ell}$, the decision between the leading two candidates reduces to:
   $$ V_{k\ell}(F_t) = p_{k\ell}^{(t)\top} \mu(s^{(t)}) + (p_{k\ell}^{(t)})_{F_t}^\top \big(x_{F_t}^{(t)} - \mu_{F_t}(s^{(t)})\big) $$
3. If pair $(k, \ell)$ is **misunderstood** on feature $j$ ($p_{k\ell, j}^{(0)} d_{k\ell, j} < 0$), the student's choice flips from $\ell$ to $k$ once exposure count $m_j$ satisfies:
   $$ \gamma^{m_j} < \frac{|d_{k\ell, j}|}{|d_{k\ell, j}| + |p_{k\ell, j}^{(0)}|} $$
4. Because this flip condition occurs at different exposure counts for different pairs $(k, \ell)$, $\mathcal{A}$ observes multiple independent flip points across margins from the same feature $j$. This yields faster convergence to $\hat{\gamma}$ with rate $\mathcal{O}(1 / \sqrt{K \cdot t})$.

### 4. Adaptive Graduation: The Dynamic Stopping Time $\tau$

In real-world tutoring, the training horizon $T$ is not an exogenous constant. $\mathcal{A}$ must determine when the student has acquired sufficient proficiency to graduate.

#### The Dual Optimization Formulation
Let $\tau$ be a stopping time with respect to the filtration $\mathcal{F}_t = \sigma(x^{(1)}, s^{(1)}, F_1, \hat{y}_1, \dots, x^{(t)}, s^{(t)}, F_t, \hat{y}_t)$.
The adaptive curriculum objective balances cumulative training mistakes with post-graduation regret:
$$ \min_{\pi, \tau} \mathbb{E}\left[ \sum_{t=1}^\tau \lambda \ell_{\text{train}}(x^{(t)}, F_t; H^{(t)}) \;+\; \frac{\delta^\tau}{1-\delta} \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(H^{(\tau)}\big) \right] $$
where $\lambda \in [0, 1]$ is the cost of mistakes during training, and $\delta \in (0, 1)$ is the graduation discount factor.

#### Analytical Marginal Pedagogical Gain
By [[<G> - 3. Multi-Action Graduation Regret]], the graduation regret under belief matrix $H$ is bounded by the active-contender sum over polyhedral cones $\mathcal{S}_{k\ell}$:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(H) \;\le\; \sum_{1 \le k < \ell \le K} P(s \in \mathcal{S}_{k\ell}) \sqrt{\frac{d_{k\ell}^\top \Sigma d_{k\ell}}{2\pi}} \Big(1 - \rho_{k\ell}\big(F_{k\ell}^*, p_{k\ell}\big)\Big) $$
where $F_{k\ell}^* = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} (p_{k\ell})_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} (p_{k\ell})_F$.

For any candidate feature $j \in [n]$, define the **Marginal Pedagogical Gain** $\Delta_j(t)$ as the reduction in expected unassisted graduation regret achieved by exposing feature $j$ at round $t$:
$$ \Delta_j(t) := \sum_{1 \le k < \ell \le K} P(s \in \mathcal{S}_{k\ell}) \sqrt{\frac{d_{k\ell}^\top \Sigma d_{k\ell}}{2\pi}} \left[ \rho_{k\ell}\big(F_{k\ell}^*, p_{k\ell}(m_j + 1)\big) - \rho_{k\ell}\big(F_{k\ell}^*, p_{k\ell}(m_j)\big) \right] $$

#### The Optimal Stopping Rule
**Theorem 2 (Adaptive Graduation Boundary):**
Under the active-contender approximation, the optimal stopping policy $\tau^*$ terminates training at the earliest round $t$ satisfying:
$$ \max_{j \in [n]} \Delta_j(t) \;\le\; \frac{1-\delta}{\delta} \lambda \mathbb{E}_{x \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma)}\big[\ell_{\text{train}}(x, F^*; H^{(t)})\big] $$

**Intuition:** $\mathcal{A}$ halts lessons and graduates the student at the exact moment when the maximum possible future debiasing benefit from another hint ($\max_j \Delta_j$) drops below the immediate expected cost of subjecting the student to further training mistakes.

### 5. Summary of the Multi-Action Tutoring Algorithm (M-ATA)

```
========================================================================================
ALGORITHM: Multi-Action Adaptive Tutoring (M-ATA)
========================================================================================
Input: Known ground truth U, budget c, training penalty λ, discount factor δ, tolerance ϵ.

PHASE 1: UNASSISTED DIAGNOSTIC PLACEMENT TEST (t = 1 to T_diag)
  1. For each diagnostic prompt:
     - Generate case x^(t), display observation s^(t) = B x^(t).
     - Set F_t = ∅ (no hints).
     - Record student decision y^_t ∈ [K].
  2. Compute initial normalized belief matrix H^(0) by solving multiclass margin loss.

PHASE 2: ADAPTIVE SCAFFOLDING & CONTRACTION TRACKING (t = T_diag + 1, ...)
  1. On round t, observe s^(t). Identify active contender cone S_kl.
  2. Evaluate marginal pedagogical gain Δ_j(t) for all j ∈ [n].
  3. STOPPING CHECK:
     - If max_j Δ_j(t) ≤ ((1 - δ)/δ) * λ * E[l_train], TRIGGER GRADUATION (τ = t), EXIT LOOP.
  4. HINT SELECTION:
     - Select F_t = argmax_{|F| ≤ c} [ λ * Immediate_Safety(F) + (δ / (1-δ)) * Contraction_Gain(F) ].
     - Reveal x_{F_t}^(t).
  5. BELIEF UPDATE & LEARNING RATE ESTIMATION:
     - Observe decision y^_t.
     - Update belief contraction parameter estimate \hat{γ}.
     - Increment exposure counts m_j(t+1) = m_j(t) + 1 for j ∈ F_t.

PHASE 3: GRADUATION (t > τ)
  - Student operates autonomously without hints.
  - Student selects F^*(s^(t); H^(τ)) = argmax_{|F| ≤ c} (p_{kl})_F^T Γ_FF (p_{kl})_F.
  - Expected regret bounded by L_grad(H^(τ)).
========================================================================================
```
