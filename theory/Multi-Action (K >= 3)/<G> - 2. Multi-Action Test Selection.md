We examine how $\mathcal{H}$ selects the feature subset $F^*(s; H)$ to reveal at graduation ($t > T$) for general $K \ge 2$, capacity $c \ge 1$, and correlated features with residual covariance $\Gamma \succeq 0$.

##### **Prior State Estimation:**
Given observation $s = Bx \in \mathbb{R}^d$, the conditional distribution of latent state $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is:
$$ x \mid s \sim \mathcal{N}\big(\mu(s), \; \Gamma\big) $$
where $\mu(s) = \Sigma B^\top (B \Sigma B^\top)^{-1} s = \Pi x$ with projection matrix $\Pi := \Sigma B^\top (B \Sigma B^\top)^{-1} B \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$, and residual covariance $\Gamma = \Sigma - \Sigma B^\top (B \Sigma B^\top)^{-1} B \Sigma \succeq 0$.

Before any additional features are revealed, the prior expected utility held by $\mathcal{H}$ for action $k \in [K]$ is:
$$ \bar{u}_k(s) := \mathbb{E}_{x \mid s}\big[ h_k^\top x \big] = h_k^\top \mu(s) $$
Let $k_0(s) := \arg\max_{k \in [K]} \bar{u}_k(s)$ denote the prior optimal action. For each action $k \in [K]$, we define the prior deficit relative to the leader:
$$ \Delta_k(s) := \bar{u}_{k_0}(s) - \bar{u}_k(s) = (h_{k_0} - h_k)^\top \mu(s) \ge 0 $$
Note that $\Delta_{k_0}(s) = 0$.

##### **Post-Test Utility and Innovation:**
When a feature subset $F \subseteq [n]$ with $|F| \le c$ is revealed, $\mathcal{H}$ observes true values $x_F$ and imputes conditional expectations $\mu_{j}(s)$ for unobserved features $j \notin F$:
$$ 
\begin{align}
\hat{u}_k(F, x_F, s; H) &= \sum_{j \in F} H_{kj} x_j + \sum_{j \notin F} H_{kj} \mu_j(s) \\
&= h_k^\top \mu(s) + h_{k, F}^\top \big(x_F - \mu_F(s)\big) \\
&= \bar{u}_k(s) + \tilde{u}_k(F)
\end{align}
$$
where $z := x - \mu(s) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Gamma)$, and $\tilde{u}_k(F) := h_{k, F}^\top z_F$ is the zero-mean Gaussian innovation.

##### **Value of Information (VoI):**
The perceived Value of Information evaluates the expected gain from observing features $F$ over acting solely on observation $s$:
$$ \operatorname{VoI}(F \mid s; H) := \mathbb{E}_{x_F \mid s}\left[ \max_{k \in [K]} \hat{u}_k(F, x_F, s; H) \right] - \max_{k \in [K]} \bar{u}_k(s) $$
The human's autonomous test selection rule is:
$$ F_{\mathcal{H}}^*(s; H) = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} \operatorname{VoI}(F \mid s; H) $$

**Claim 1 (Exact Relative Representation):**
The Value of Information can be expressed exactly as:
$$ \operatorname{VoI}(F \mid s; H) = \mathbb{E}_{z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Gamma)}\left[ \max\left( 0, \; \max_{k \neq k_0} \big( Z_k(F) - \Delta_k(s) \big) \right) \right] $$
where $Z_k(F) := \tilde{u}_k(F) - \tilde{u}_{k_0}(F) = (h_k - h_{k_0})_F^\top z_F \sim \mathcal{N}\big(0, \; \sigma_k^2(F)\big)$ with resolved variance:
$$ \sigma_k^2(F) = (h_k - h_{k_0})_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} (h_k - h_{k_0})_F $$

**Proof:**
Using the definition of prior leader $k_0$, we rewrite the post-test maximum:
$$
\begin{align}
\max_{k \in [K]} \hat{u}_k(F) - \bar{u}_{k_0}(s) &= \max_{k \in [K]} \big( \bar{u}_k(s) - \bar{u}_{k_0}(s) + \tilde{u}_k(F) \big) \\
&= \max_{k \in [K]} \big( \tilde{u}_k(F) - \Delta_k(s) \big)
\end{align}
$$
Separating $k = k_0$ (where $\Delta_{k_0}(s) = 0$) from $k \neq k_0$:
$$ \max_{k \in [K]} \big( \tilde{u}_k(F) - \Delta_k(s) \big) = \max\left( \tilde{u}_{k_0}(F), \; \max_{k \neq k_0} \big(\tilde{u}_k(F) - \Delta_k(s)\big) \right) $$
Subtracting $\tilde{u}_{k_0}(F)$ from both terms inside the maximum:
$$ = \tilde{u}_{k_0}(F) + \max\left( 0, \; \max_{k \neq k_0} \big( \tilde{u}_k(F) - \tilde{u}_{k_0}(F) - \Delta_k(s) \big) \right) $$
Since $\tilde{u}_{k_0}(F) = h_{k_0, F}^\top z_F$ and $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Gamma)$, its expectation is zero: $\mathbb{E}[\tilde{u}_{k_0}(F)] = 0$. Taking expectations across both sides yields the claim. $\blacksquare$

---

**Fact 1 (Gaussian Surplus Function / Expected Improvement):**
For scalar Gaussian variable $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ and threshold $\Delta \ge 0$:
$$ \psi(\Delta, \sigma) := \mathbb{E}\big[\max(0, Z - \Delta)\big] = \sigma \, \phi\left(\frac{\Delta}{\sigma}\right) - \Delta \, \Phi\left(-\frac{\Delta}{\sigma}\right) $$
where $\phi(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-z^2/2}$ is the standard normal PDF and $\Phi(z)$ is the standard normal CDF.
* For any fixed $\Delta \ge 0$, $\psi(\Delta, \sigma)$ is strictly increasing in $\sigma > 0$.
* When $\Delta = 0$, $\psi(0, \sigma) = \sigma \phi(0) = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{2\pi}} = \frac{1}{2} \mathbb{E}[|Z|]$.
* For large threshold ratios $\frac{\Delta}{\sigma} \gg 1$, Mill's ratio gives exponential decay: $\psi(\Delta, \sigma) \le \frac{\sigma^3}{\Delta^2 \sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\Delta^2 / (2\sigma^2)}$.

---

**Theorem 1 (Exact VoI Sandwich Bounds):**
For any $K \ge 2$, $c \ge 1$, $\Gamma \succeq 0$, and observation $s$:
$$ \max_{k \neq k_0} \psi\big(\Delta_k(s), \sigma_k(F)\big) \;\le\; \operatorname{VoI}(F \mid s; H) \;\le\; \sum_{k \neq k_0} \psi\big(\Delta_k(s), \sigma_k(F)\big) $$

**Proof:**
1. **Lower Bound:** For any competitor $k \neq k_0$, $\max(0, \max_{j \neq k_0}(Z_j - \Delta_j)) \ge \max(0, Z_k - \Delta_k)$. Taking expectations:
   $$ \operatorname{VoI}(F \mid s; H) \ge \mathbb{E}\big[\max(0, Z_k - \Delta_k)\big] = \psi\big(\Delta_k(s), \sigma_k(F)\big) $$
   Since this holds for every $k \neq k_0$, taking the maximum over $k \neq k_0$ gives the lower bound.
2. **Upper Bound:** Using the sub-additivity of the positive part $\max(0, \max_j A_j) \le \sum_j \max(0, A_j)$:
   $$ \max\left( 0, \; \max_{k \neq k_0}(Z_k - \Delta_k) \right) \le \sum_{k \neq k_0} \max(0, Z_k - \Delta_k) $$
   Taking expectations across the sum:
   $$ \operatorname{VoI}(F \mid s; H) \le \sum_{k \neq k_0} \mathbb{E}\big[\max(0, Z_k - \Delta_k)\big] = \sum_{k \neq k_0} \psi\big(\Delta_k(s), \sigma_k(F)\big) \quad \blacksquare $$

---

##### **Implications:**

1. **Exact Recovery of $K = 2$:**  
   When $K = 2$, there is only one competitor $k_1 \neq k_0$. The upper and lower bounds are identical:
   $$ \max_{k \neq k_0} \psi\big(\Delta_k, \sigma_k\big) = \psi\big(\Delta_1, \sigma_1\big) = \sum_{k \neq k_0} \psi\big(\Delta_k, \sigma_k\big) $$
   The sandwich is tight with zero gap. Maximizing $\psi(\Delta_1(s), \sigma_1(F))$ is equivalent to maximizing $\sigma_1^2(F) = p_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_F$, recovering the exact theorem in [[2. Test Selection.md]].

2. **Active Contender Pinching:**  
   Let $k^*(s) = \arg\min_{k \neq k_0} \Delta_k(s)$ be the primary competitor. If all other competitors have $\Delta_k(s) \ge 3 \sigma_k(F)$, their surplus satisfies $\psi(\Delta_k, \sigma_k) \le 0.001 \sigma_k \approx 0$.
   The upper and lower bounds converge, pinching $\operatorname{VoI}(F \mid s; H)$ to the primary margin:
   $$ \operatorname{VoI}(F \mid s; H) = \psi\Big(\Delta_{k^*}(s), \; \sqrt{(h_{k_0} - h_{k^*})_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} (h_{k_0} - h_{k^*})_F}\Big) + \mathcal{O}\left( \sum_{k \notin \{k_0, k^*\}} e^{-\Delta_k^2 / 2\sigma_k^2} \right) $$
   In this regime, the feature selection rule simplifies to:
   $$ F^*(s) = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} (h_{k_0} - h_{k^*})_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} (h_{k_0} - h_{k^*})_F $$

3. **Smooth Softmax Representation:**  
   When multiple competitors are simultaneously close ($\Delta_k(s) \approx 0$), approximating $\max_k u_k$ by the smooth Log-Sum-Exp $M_\beta(u) = \frac{1}{\beta}\log \sum_k e^{\beta u_k}$ and expanding to second order around $\bar{u}(s)$ yields:
   $$ \operatorname{VoI}(F \mid s; H) \approx \frac{\beta}{2} \sum_{1 \le k < \ell \le K} \pi_k(s) \pi_\ell(s) \cdot (h_k - h_\ell)_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} (h_k - h_\ell)_F = \frac{\beta}{2} z^\top \big( \Gamma \odot M(s) \big) z $$
   where $z \in \{0, 1\}^n$ is the indicator vector of $F$, $\pi_k(s) = \frac{e^{\beta \bar{u}_k(s)}}{\sum_r e^{\beta \bar{u}_r(s)}}$ is the softmax probability, and $M(s) := \sum_{k < \ell} \pi_k(s) \pi_\ell(s) (h_k - h_\ell)(h_k - h_\ell)^\top \succeq 0$.
