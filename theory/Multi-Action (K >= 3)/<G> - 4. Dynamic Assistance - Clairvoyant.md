We analyze multi-action dynamic assistance ($K \ge 2$) when training decisions carry real-world cost ($\lambda > 0$). In this module, we develop the theoretical offline benchmarks where the sequence of training cases is known in advance: the **Full Oracle** (where latent states $x^{(1)}, \dots, x^{(T)}$ are known) and the **Symptom-Based Clairvoyant** (where presenting observations $s^{(1)}, \dots, s^{(T)}$ are known).

---

### 1. Multi-Action Dynamic Problem Formulation ($\lambda > 0$)

Nature generates a sequence of $T$ training instances: $x^{(t)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ with observations $s^{(t)} = Bx^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and conditional distribution $x^{(t)} \mid s^{(t)} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu(s^{(t)}), \Gamma)$, where:
$$ \mu(s) = \Pi x \quad \text{with } \Pi = \Sigma B^\top (B \Sigma B^\top)^{-1} B, \qquad \Gamma = \Sigma - \Sigma B^\top (B \Sigma B^\top)^{-1} B \Sigma \succeq 0 $$

The ground truth utility matrix is $U \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times n}$ with rows $u_1, \dots, u_K$. For state $x^{(t)}$, the optimal action is $y^*(x^{(t)}) = \arg\max_{k \in [K]} u_k^\top x^{(t)}$.

At each training round $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$:
1. Algorithm $\mathcal{A}$ selects a feature subset $F_t \subseteq [n]$ with $|F_t| \le c$ to reveal to $\mathcal{H}$.
2. True feature values $x_{F_t}^{(t)}$ are revealed. $\mathcal{H}$ imputes conditional expectations $\mu_j(s^{(t)})$ for unmeasured features $j \notin F_t$.
   The perceived utility for action $k \in [K]$ held by $\mathcal{H}$ is:
   $$ \hat{u}_k(F_t, x_{F_t}^{(t)}, s^{(t)}; H^{(t)}) = h_k^{(t)\top} \mu(s^{(t)}) + h_{k, F_t}^{(t)\top} \big(x_{F_t}^{(t)} - \mu_{F_t}(s^{(t)})\big) $$
   where $H^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times n}$ is $\mathcal{H}$'s belief matrix at round $t$.
3. $\mathcal{H}$ executes action $\hat{y}_t = \arg\max_{k \in [K]} \hat{u}_k(F_t, x_{F_t}^{(t)}, s^{(t)}; H^{(t)})$.
4. Decision regret suffered on round $t$ is:
   $$ \ell_{\text{train}}(x^{(t)}, \hat{y}_t) = \max_{k \in [K]} u_k^\top x^{(t)} - u_{\hat{y}_t}^\top x^{(t)} = (u_{y^*(x^{(t)})} - u_{\hat{y}_t})^\top x^{(t)} $$
5. **Belief Matrix Contraction:**
   For all revealed features $j \in F_t$, beliefs contract across all action rows $k \in [K]$:
   $$ H_{kj}^{(t+1)} - U_{kj} = \psi(m_j(t) + 1) \big[ H_{kj}^{(0)} - U_{kj} \big] \quad \forall (k, j) \in [K] \times F_t $$
   where $m_j(t)$ is the cumulative exposure count of feature $j$ prior to round $t$. For unrevealed features $j \notin F_t$, beliefs remain frozen: $H_{kj}^{(t+1)} = H_{kj}^{(t)}$.

##### **The Dual Multi-Action Objective:**
The goal of the algorithm $\mathcal{A}$ is to select the sequence of feature panels $\pi = (F_1, \dots, F_T)$ to minimize:
$$ \mathcal{L}(\pi) := \lambda \sum_{t=1}^T \ell_{\text{train}}(x^{(t)}, \hat{y}_t) \;+\; \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(H^{(T+1)}\big) $$
where from [[<G> - 3. Multi-Action Graduation Regret.md]], the expected graduation regret is:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(H) = \sum_{1 \le k < \ell \le K} w_{k\ell} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{d_{k\ell}^\top \Sigma d_{k\ell}}{2\pi}} \Big(1 - \rho_{k\ell}\big(F_{k\ell}^*, p_{k\ell}\big)\Big) $$
with $w_{k\ell} = \mathbb{P}(s \in \mathcal{S}_{k\ell})$, $d_{k\ell} = u_k - u_\ell$, $p_{k\ell} = h_k - h_\ell$, and $F_{k\ell}^* = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} (p_{k\ell})_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} (p_{k\ell})_F$.
*Caveat (review 2026-11-06):* this product form is a heuristic surrogate, not an identity — see the corrected Theorem 2 of [[<G> - 3. Multi-Action Graduation Regret]]. The DPs below are exact for whatever terminal cost is plugged in; use the exact $\mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(H) = \mathbb{E}[\ell_{\text{grad}}]$ (e.g. Monte Carlo) when exactness matters.

### 2. The Multi-Margin Dynamic Coupling Challenge

In the multi-action setting, training decisions are linked across actions by two structural mechanisms:

1. **Cross-Margin Feature Sharing:**  
   Revealing a feature $j \in F_t$ contracts column $j$ of belief matrix $H$. This simultaneously modifies the perceived difference vector:
   $$ p_{k\ell, j}(m_j + 1) = (1 - \psi(m_j + 1)) d_{k\ell, j} + \psi(m_j + 1) p_{k\ell, j}^{(0)} $$
   across **all $\binom{K}{2}$ action pairs simultaneously**.
2. **Symptom-Driven Contender Activation:**  
   Unlike $K=2$ where the same single margin $(1, 2)$ is always active, observation $s^{(t)}$ activates a specific subset of contending actions.
   Let prior expected utilities be $\bar{u}_k(s^{(t)}) = h_k^{(t)\top} \mu(s^{(t)})$. The symptoms $s^{(t)}$ isolate the primary competing margin:
   $$ k_0(s^{(t)}) = \arg\max_{k \in [K]} \bar{u}_k(s^{(t)}), \qquad k^*(s^{(t)}) = \arg\max_{k \neq k_0} \bar{u}_k(s^{(t)}) $$
   A panel $F_t$ chosen to separate $k_0$ from $k^*$ on patient $t$ concurrently updates beliefs on all other margins sharing features with $F_t$.

### 3. Regime 1: The Full Oracle Benchmark (Known $x_{1:T}, s_{1:T}$)

In the Full Oracle regime, $\mathcal{A}$ has complete foresight of all realized latent states $x^{(1)}, \dots, x^{(T)}$ and observations $s^{(1)}, \dots, s^{(T)}$ before round 1 begins.

##### **Deterministic Stage Evaluation:**
Because $x^{(t)}$ is known, the optimal action $y^*(x^{(t)})$ is deterministic.
For any candidate feature subset $F \subseteq [n]$ ($|F| \le c$) and exposure count vector $m \in \mathbb{N}^n$, the perceived utilities evaluate to exact deterministic values:
$$ \hat{u}_k(F, x_F^{(t)}, s^{(t)}; H(m)) = h_k(m)^\top \mu(s^{(t)}) + h_{k, F}(m)^\top \big(x_F^{(t)} - \mu_F(s^{(t)})\big) $$
The resulting human action is deterministic:
$$ \hat{y}(F, x^{(t)}; m) = \arg\max_{k \in [K]} \hat{u}_k(F, x_F^{(t)}, s^{(t)}; H(m)) $$
The stage loss evaluates to a known quantity:
$$ g_t(m, F) := \lambda \Big( \max_{k \in [K]} u_k^\top x^{(t)} - u_{\hat{y}(F, x^{(t)}; m)}^\top x^{(t)} \Big) \ge 0 $$

##### **Definition: Multi-Action Zero-Regret Set:**
For state $x^{(t)}$ and observation $s^{(t)}$, given current belief $H(m)$, the multi-action zero-regret set is:
$$ Z(x^{(t)}, s^{(t)}; H(m)) := \Big\{ F \subseteq [n], |F| \le c : \hat{y}(F, x^{(t)}; m) = y^*(x^{(t)}) \Big\} $$
Whenever $F_t \in Z(x^{(t)}, s^{(t)}; H(m))$, the stage loss is identically zero: $g_t(m, F_t) = 0$.

##### **Formulation as an Exact Finite-Horizon Dynamic Program:**
* **State Space:** Cumulative exposure vector $m(t) = (m_1, \dots, m_n) \in \mathbb{N}^n$ with $\sum_{j=1}^n m_j(t) = c(t-1)$.
* **Control:** Feature panel $F_t \subseteq [n]$ with $|F_t| \le c$.
* **State Transition:** $m(t+1) = m(t) + \sum_{j \in F_t} e_j$.
* **Terminal Value ($t = T+1$):**
  $$ J_{T+1}(m) = \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(H(m)\big) = \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \sum_{1 \le k < \ell \le K} w_{k\ell} \sqrt{\frac{d_{k\ell}^\top \Sigma d_{k\ell}}{2\pi}} \Big(1 - \rho_{k\ell}\big(F_{k\ell}^*, p_{k\ell}(m)\big)\Big) $$
* **Bellman Backward Recursion:**
  $$ J_t(m) = \min_{F \subseteq [n], |F| \le c} \Big\{ g_t(m, F) + J_{t+1}\big(m + e_F\big) \Big\} \quad \text{for } t = T, T-1, \dots, 1 $$
  where $e_F := \sum_{j \in F} e_j$.

##### **The Oracle Teachable Moment Matching Theorem:**
\begin{theorem}[Oracle Zero-Cost Debiasing — corrected]
On round $t$ at counts $m$, the Oracle prefers a zero-cost panel $F \in Z(x^{(t)}, s^{(t)}; H(m))$ to any panel $F'$ iff $J_{t+1}(m + e_F) \le g_t(m, F') + J_{t+1}(m + e_{F'})$. In particular, if some $F \in Z$ attains $\min_{|F'| \le c} J_{t+1}(m + e_{F'})$, the Oracle teaches it on round $t$ at zero immediate regret.
\end{theorem}

*Proof:* Bellman recursion with $g_t(m, F) = 0$ for $F \in Z$ and $g_t \ge 0$ otherwise. $\blacksquare$

*(The earlier statement — "the Oracle assigns $F_t = F^*$ whenever $F^* \in Z$" — was false: another zero-cost panel may have a better continuation, and $J_{t+1}$ is not monotone in exposures, since exposures can hand salience to a misunderstood feature.)*

The Full Oracle effectively matches the $\binom{K}{2}$ pairwise misconceptions in $H^{(0)}$ to the specific instances in the cohort where natural variation renders those features safe to expose.

### 4. Regime 2: The Symptom-Based Clairvoyant (Known $s_{1:T}$)

In the Symptom-Based Clairvoyant regime, $\mathcal{A}$ knows the cohort sequence of presenting observations $s^{(1)}, \dots, s^{(T)}$ in advance, but the underlying state $x^{(t)}$ is unobserved prior to round $t$:
$$ x^{(t)} \mid s^{(t)} \sim \mathcal{N}\big(\mu(s^{(t)}), \; \Gamma\big) $$

##### **Expected Multi-Action Immediate Regret Given $s^{(t)}$:**
For candidate panel $F$ and belief matrix $H(m)$, the expected immediate regret on patient $t$ given symptoms $s^{(t)}$ is:
$$ R(F \mid s^{(t)}; H(m)) := \mathbb{E}_{x \mid s^{(t)}}\left[ \max_{k \in [K]} u_k^\top x - u_{\hat{y}(F, x_F, s^{(t)}; H(m))}^\top x \right] $$

**Claim 1 (Active Contender Decomposition of Immediate Risk):**
Let $k_0 = \arg\max_k h_k(m)^\top \mu(s^{(t)})$ and $k^* = \arg\max_{k \neq k_0} h_k(m)^\top \mu(s^{(t)})$ be the top two contenders under $\mathcal{H}$'s prior on instance $t$.
When non-contending actions have prior deficits $\Delta_r(s^{(t)}) := (h_{k_0} - h_r)^\top \mu(s^{(t)}) \ge 3 \sigma_r(F)$, the multi-action immediate risk collapses to the active pair $(k_0, k^*)$:
$$ R(F \mid s^{(t)}; H(m)) = R_{k_0, k^*}\big(F \mid s^{(t)}; p_{k_0 k^*}(m)\big) + \mathcal{O}\left( \sum_{r \notin \{k_0, k^*\}} e^{-\Delta_r(s^{(t)})^2 / 2\sigma_r(F)^2} \right) $$
where $R_{k_0, k^*}$ is evaluated by the non-central bivariate Gaussian orthant integral from [[<G> - 5. Dynamic Assistance (Lambda > 0).md]]:
$$ R_{k_0, k^*}(F \mid s^{(t)}; p) = \sigma_D \int_{-\infty}^\infty \phi(w) \cdot \Big| w + \frac{\mu_D}{\sigma_D} \Big| \cdot \Phi\left( -\operatorname{sgn}\Big(w + \frac{\mu_D}{\sigma_D}\Big) \cdot \frac{\mu_V / \sigma_V + \tilde{\rho}_{k_0 k^*}(F) w}{\sqrt{1 - \tilde{\rho}_{k_0 k^*}(F)^2}} \right) dw $$
with $\mu_D = (u_{k_0} - u_{k^*})^\top \mu(s^{(t)})$, $\mu_V = (h_{k_0} - h_{k^*})^\top \mu(s^{(t)})$, $\sigma_D = \sqrt{d_{k_0 k^*}^\top \Gamma d_{k_0 k^*}}$, $\sigma_V = \sqrt{p_{k_0 k^*, F}^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_{k_0 k^*, F}}$, and correlation $\tilde{\rho}_{k_0 k^*}(F) = \frac{p_{k_0 k^*, F}^\top \Gamma_{F, :} d_{k_0 k^*}}{\sigma_D \sigma_V}$.

##### **The Symptom Clairvoyant Stochastic Dynamic Program:**
The preceptor solves the backward dynamic program across the known symptom cohort:
$$ J_t(m) = \min_{F \subseteq [n], |F| \le c} \Big\{ \lambda R\big(F \mid s^{(t)}; H(m)\big) + J_{t+1}(m + e_F) \Big\} \quad \text{for } t = T, \dots, 1 $$
with terminal condition:
$$ J_{T+1}(m) = \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(H(m)\big) $$

##### **Cohort Scheduling Algorithm for Known Symptoms:**
Knowing $s^{(1)}, \dots, s^{(T)}$ in advance allows $\mathcal{A}$ to optimize the sequence of interventions across the patient cohort:
1. **Margin Identification:** For each round $t$, identify the active competing margin $(k_0(s^{(t)}), k^*(s^{(t)}))$ and its stakes $|\mu_D(s^{(t)})|$.
2. **Defensive Assignment:** Reserve high-stakes rounds ($|\mu_D| \gg 0$ with misaligned $\mu_V$) for panels that maximize local correlation $\tilde{\rho}_{k_0 k^*}(F) \approx 1$.
3. **Opportunistic Debiasing:** Schedule uncalibrated features on rounds where symptoms naturally separate the contenders ($\mu_D \cdot \mu_V \gg 0$), so that $R(F \mid s^{(t)}) \approx 0$ regardless of $F$.

### 5. Theoretical Comparison Across Regimes

\begin{theorem}[The Multi-Action Pedagogical Information Hierarchy]
Let $\mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{oracle}})$ and $\mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{symptom}})$ denote the total expected loss under the optimal Full Oracle and Symptom Clairvoyant policies, respectively. For any $\lambda > 0$, budget $T$, and initial belief matrix $H^{(0)}$:
$$ \mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{oracle}}) \;\le\; \mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{symptom}}) $$
Furthermore, for fixed $\delta$, as $\lambda \to 0$:
$$ \lim_{\lambda \to 0} \mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{oracle}}) = \lim_{\lambda \to 0} \mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{symptom}}) = \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(H(m^*)\big) $$
(If $\delta$ also moves, the right side moves with it; state the limit for the normalized loss $\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}\mathcal{L}$, which tends to $\mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(H(m^*))$ as $\lambda(1-\delta)/\delta \to 0$.)
where $m^* \in \mathbb{N}^n$ is the optimal static curriculum allocation minimizing graduation regret subject to $\sum_{j=1}^n m_j \le cT$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
1. The Symptom Clairvoyant chooses $F_t$ based on filtration $\mathcal{F}_t^{\text{sym}} = \sigma(s^{(1)}, \dots, s^{(T)})$, whereas the Full Oracle chooses $F_t$ based on $\mathcal{F}_t^{\text{state}} = \sigma(x^{(1)}, \dots, x^{(T)})$. Because $s^{(t)} = Bx^{(t)}$, $\mathcal{F}_t^{\text{sym}} \subset \mathcal{F}_t^{\text{state}}$. Any policy feasible under $\mathcal{F}^{\text{sym}}$ is feasible under $\mathcal{F}^{\text{state}}$. Taking the infimum over a larger policy class yields $\mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{oracle}}) \le \mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{symptom}})$.
2. Every policy has $\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(H(m(T+1))) \le \mathcal{L}(\pi) \le \lambda T \bar{\ell} + \frac{\delta}{1-\delta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(H(m(T+1)))$, with $\bar{\ell}$ the largest expected per-round regret. The terminal term depends only on $m(T+1)$, and the minimizing allocation $m^*$ (budget $\sum_j m_j \le cT$, $m_j \le T$) is reachable by a fixed schedule under either information structure. Hence both optima are squeezed to $\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(H(m^*))$ as $\lambda \to 0$. $\blacksquare$
\end{proof}
