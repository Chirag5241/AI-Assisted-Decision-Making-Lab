We consider the general multi-action decision setting with $K \ge 2$ competing actions, latent state $x \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma) \in \mathbb{R}^n$, observation $s = Bx \in \mathbb{R}^d$, and feature reveal budget $|F| \le c$.

##### **Reduction to Pairwise Difference Vectors:**
The ground truth utility matrix is $U \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times n}$ with rows $u_1, \dots, u_K \in \mathbb{R}^n$. 
For any pair of actions $k, \ell \in [K]$ ($k \neq \ell$), we define the ground truth pairwise difference vector:
$$ d_{k\ell} := u_k - u_\ell \in \mathbb{R}^n $$
At time $t$, the human maintains internal belief matrix $H^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times n}$ with rows $h_1^{(t)}, \dots, h_K^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^n$. We define the perceived pairwise difference vector:
$$ p_{k\ell}^{(t)} := h_k^{(t)} - h_\ell^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^n $$
Note that $d_{\ell k} = -d_{k\ell}$ and $p_{\ell k}^{(t)} = -p_{k\ell}^{(t)}$. There are $\binom{K}{2} = \frac{K(K-1)}{2}$ unique undirected action pairs.

For state $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$, action $k$ is optimal ($y^*(x) = k$) iff it dominates all competitors:
$$ y^*(x) = \arg\max_{k \in [K]} u_k^\top x \iff d_{k\ell}^\top x \ge 0 \quad \forall \ell \in [K] $$
When an action $\hat{y} \in [K]$ is selected, the decision regret incurred is:
$$ \ell_{\text{reg}}(x, \hat{y}) = \max_{k \in [K]} u_k^\top x - u_{\hat{y}}^\top x = d_{y^*(x), \hat{y}}^\top x $$

##### **Learning Dynamics Across Action Margins:**
At each training round $t \le T$, the algorithm $\mathcal{A}$ selects a subset of features $F_t \subseteq [n]$ with $|F_t| \le c$ to reveal to $\mathcal{H}$. 
By `Problem Description.md`, beliefs contract for all revealed features across all actions:
$$ H_{kj}^{(t+1)} - U_{kj} = \psi(m_j(t)) \big[ H_{kj}^{(0)} - U_{kj} \big] \quad \forall (k, j) \in [K] \times F_t $$
where $m_j(t) = |\{ \tau < t : j \in F_\tau \}|$ is the cumulative exposure count of feature $j$, and $\psi: \mathbb{N} \to [0, 1]$ is non-increasing with $\psi(0) = 1$ and $\lim_{m \to \infty} \psi(m) = 0$. For unrevealed features $j \notin F_t$, beliefs remain unchanged.

Subtracting row $\ell$ from row $k$, the perceived difference vector along margin $(k, \ell)$ evolves deterministically as:
$$ p_{k\ell, j}(m_j) = (1 - \psi(m_j)) d_{k\ell, j} + \psi(m_j) p_{k\ell, j}^{(0)} $$
where $m_j := m_j(t)$.

##### **Pairwise Feature Classification:**
Unlike the binary case where each feature has a single identity, in the multi-action setting each feature $j \in [n]$ has a specific role **relative to each action pair $(k, \ell)$**:

1. **Informative for $(k, \ell)$**: $d_{k\ell, j} \neq 0$ and $p_{k\ell, j}^{(0)} d_{k\ell, j} > 0$.
   * $\mathcal{H}$ correctly understands the sign of the distinction between action $k$ and $\ell$ along feature $j$. Since $\psi(m_j) \in [0, 1]$, $p_{k\ell, j}(m_j) d_{k\ell, j} > 0$ for all $m_j \ge 0$.
2. **Misunderstood for $(k, \ell)$**: $d_{k\ell, j} \neq 0$ and $p_{k\ell, j}^{(0)} d_{k\ell, j} < 0$.
   * $\mathcal{H}$ initially believes feature $j$ favors action $\ell$ when it actually favors action $k$. Under exposures $m_j$, the belief contracts toward $d_{k\ell, j}$ and becomes informative ($p_{k\ell, j}(m_j) d_{k\ell, j} > 0$) once:
     $$ \psi(m_j) < \frac{|d_{k\ell, j}|}{|p_{k\ell, j}^{(0)}| + |d_{k\ell, j}|} $$
3. **Distraction for $(k, \ell)$**: $d_{k\ell, j} = 0$ and $|p_{k\ell, j}^{(0)}| > 0$.
   * Feature $j$ is irrelevant for distinguishing action $k$ from action $\ell$, but $\mathcal{H}$ mistakenly relies on it. Its perceived weight decays as $p_{k\ell, j}(m_j) = \psi(m_j) p_{k\ell, j}^{(0)}$.

##### **The Cross-Margin Coupling Phenomenon:**
In the multi-action setting, features are coupled across margins:
* A single feature $j$ may be **informative** for pair $(1, 2)$ ($d_{12, j} > 0, p_{12, j}^{(0)} > 0$), **misunderstood** for pair $(2, 3)$ ($d_{23, j} > 0, p_{23, j}^{(0)} < 0$), and a **distraction** for pair $(1, 3)$ ($d_{13, j} = 0, p_{13, j}^{(0)} \neq 0$).
* Exposing feature $j$ reduces miscalibration across **all $\binom{K}{2}$ margins simultaneously**.
* The curriculum allocation problem must therefore coordinate feature exposures across multiple competing margins.
