We analyze the real-time online pedagogical assistance problem for multi-action decision settings ($K \ge 2$) under training regret penalties ($\lambda > 0$). In this realistic deployment regime, the preceptor $\mathcal{A}$ observes incoming observations $s^{(t)}$ in real time and must select feature panels $F_t$ without foresight of future cases.

---

### 1. The Multi-Action Online Assistance Protocol

At each training round $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$:
1. Nature realizes latent state $x^{(t)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma) \in \mathbb{R}^n$, presenting observation $s^{(t)} = Bx^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ arrives.
2. The preceptor $\mathcal{A}$ observes:
   * The current symptom vector $s^{(t)}$,
   * The current human belief matrix $H^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times n}$ and exposure count vector $m(t-1) \in \mathbb{N}^n$,
   * The remaining training horizon $T - t + 1$.
   Crucially, $\mathcal{A}$ has **no foresight** of future cases $(s^{(\tau)})_{\tau > t}$.
3. $\mathcal{A}$ selects a feature panel $F_t \subseteq [n]$ with $|F_t| \le c$ in real time.
4. True feature values $x_{F_t}^{(t)}$ are revealed to $\mathcal{H}$. $\mathcal{H}$ executes action:
   $$ \hat{y}_t = \arg\max_{k \in [K]} \Big( h_k^{(t)\top} \mu(s^{(t)}) + h_{k, F_t}^{(t)\top} \big(x_{F_t}^{(t)} - \mu_{F_t}(s^{(t)})\big) \Big) $$
5. Immediate regret $\ell_{\text{train}}(x^{(t)}, \hat{y}_t) = \max_k u_k^\top x^{(t)} - u_{\hat{y}_t}^\top x^{(t)}$ is incurred.
6. Cumulative exposures increment: $m_j(t) = m_j(t-1) + \mathbb{1}[j \in F_t]$. Beliefs contract:
   $$ H_{kj}^{(t+1)} - U_{kj} = \psi(m_j(t)) \big[ H_{kj}^{(0)} - U_{kj} \big] \quad \forall (k, j) \in [K] \times F_t $$

The total expected cumulative loss over the training and graduation horizon is:
$$ \mathcal{L}(\pi) = \lambda \sum_{t=1}^T \mathbb{E}\big[\ell_{\text{train}}(x^{(t)}, \hat{y}_t)\big] \;+\; \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(H^{(T+1)}\big) $$

---

### 2. The Online Bellman Formulation & Tractability

Under stochastic dynamic programming, the exact optimal online policy satisfies the Bellman equation:
$$ F_t^* = \arg\min_{F \subseteq [n], |F| \le c} \left\{ \lambda R\big(F \mid s^{(t)}; H(m(t-1))\big) \;+\; \mathbb{E}_{s^{(t+1:T)}}\Big[ J_{t+1}(m(t-1) + e_F) \Big] \right\} $$
where $R(F \mid s^{(t)}; H)$ is the expected immediate regret on patient $t$ given symptoms $s^{(t)}$.

##### **The Computational Challenge:**
Evaluating the nested future expectation $\mathbb{E}_{s^{(t+1:T)}}[\dots]$ requires backward integration across an exponentially growing tree of continuous Gaussian observations, coupled with discrete non-convex argmax partitions. This is computationally intractable for real-time deployment.

To achieve real-time polynomial-time execution, we derive an index policy that approximates future continuation value via the **Marginal Multi-Margin Graduation Dividend**.

---

### 3. The Multi-Action Online Pedagogical Index

##### **Definition 1: Marginal Multi-Margin Graduation Dividend**
For current belief matrix $H(m)$ with exposure count vector $m \in \mathbb{N}^n$, the marginal graduation dividend of revealing feature subset $F \subseteq [n]$ is:
$$ \Delta \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(F; H(m)) := \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(H(m)\big) - \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(H(m + e_F)\big) $$
where from [[<G> - 3. Multi-Action Graduation Regret.md]], the expected graduation regret is:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(H(m)) = \sum_{1 \le k < \ell \le K} w_{k\ell} \sqrt{\frac{d_{k\ell}^\top \Sigma d_{k\ell}}{2\pi}} \Big(1 - \rho_{k\ell}\big(F_{k\ell}^*(p(m)), p_{k\ell}(m)\big)\Big) $$
$\Delta \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(F; H(m))$ measures the permanent reduction in expected post-graduation regret across all population margins achieved by advancing the calibration of features in $F$.

##### **Definition 2: The Multi-Action Online Pedagogical Index ($Q_t$)**
For an incoming observation $s^{(t)}$, current belief $H(m)$, and candidate panel $F$ ($|F| \le c$), we define the Online Pedagogical Index:
$$ Q_t(F \mid s^{(t)}; m) := \lambda \cdot R\big(F \mid s^{(t)}; H(m)\big) \;-\; \gamma_t \cdot \Delta \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(F; H(m)\big) $$
where:
$$ \gamma_t := \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \cdot \frac{1}{T - t + 1} $$
is the amortized shadow price of graduation learning per remaining training round.

The preceptor chooses panel:
$$ F_t^* = \arg\min_{F \subseteq [n], |F| \le c} Q_t(F \mid s^{(t)}; m(t-1)) $$

---

### 4. Real-Time Evaluation via the Active-Contender Reduction

To evaluate $R(F \mid s^{(t)}; H(m))$ in sub-millisecond real time across candidate panels, we leverage the Active-Contender Reduction established in [[<G> - 2. Multi-Action Test Selection.md]]:

1. **Identify the Leading Contenders:**
   Given $s^{(t)}$, compute prior expected utilities $\bar{u}_k(s^{(t)}) = h_k(m)^\top \mu(s^{(t)})$ for all $k \in [K]$.
   Sort actions to isolate the top two candidates:
   $$ k_0(s^{(t)}) = \arg\max_{k \in [K]} \bar{u}_k(s^{(t)}), \qquad k^*(s^{(t)}) = \arg\max_{k \neq k_0} \bar{u}_k(s^{(t)}) $$
2. **Evaluate Pairwise Immediate Risk:**
   For each candidate panel $F \subseteq [n]$ ($|F| \le c$), the immediate risk reduces to the active margin:
   $$ R(F \mid s^{(t)}; H(m)) \approx R_{k_0 k^*}\big(F \mid s^{(t)}; p_{k_0 k^*}(m)\big) $$
   where $R_{k_0 k^*}$ is given in closed form by the non-central bivariate Gaussian orthant integral:
   $$ R_{k_0 k^*}(F) = \sigma_D \int_{-\infty}^\infty \phi(w) \cdot \Big| w + \frac{\mu_D}{\sigma_D} \Big| \cdot \Phi\left( -\operatorname{sgn}\Big(w + \frac{\mu_D}{\sigma_D}\Big) \cdot \frac{\mu_V / \sigma_V + \tilde{\rho}_{k_0 k^*}(F) w}{\sqrt{1 - \tilde{\rho}_{k_0 k^*}(F)^2}} \right) dw $$
   with:
   * $\mu_D = (u_{k_0} - u_{k^*})^\top \mu(s^{(t)})$ and $\mu_V = (h_{k_0} - h_{k^*})^\top \mu(s^{(t)})$,
   * $\sigma_D = \sqrt{(u_{k_0} - u_{k^*})^\top \Gamma (u_{k_0} - u_{k^*})}$ and $\sigma_V = \sqrt{(p_{k_0 k^*})_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} (p_{k_0 k^*})_F}$,
   * Local correlation: $\tilde{\rho}_{k_0 k^*}(F) = \frac{(p_{k_0 k^*})_F^\top \Gamma_{F, :} (u_{k_0} - u_{k^*})}{\sigma_D \sigma_V}$.

### 5. Algorithm: The Multi-Action Online Preceptor (M-AOP)

```
Algorithm: Multi-Action Online Preceptor (M-AOP)
Input: Ground truth U, initial belief H^(0), budget c, discount delta, cost lambda, horizon T
Initialize: m = (0, ..., 0) in N^n, H = H^(0)

For t = 1 to T:
   1. Observe incoming symptom vector s^(t)
   2. Compute conditional mean mu(s^(t)) = Sigma B^T (B Sigma B^T)^{-1} s^(t)   (from s only; x^(t) is unseen)
   3. Compute prior expected utilities: u_bar_k = h_k^T mu(s^(t)) for all k in [K]
   4. Find active contenders:
         k0 = argmax_{k} u_bar_k
         k* = argmax_{k != k0} u_bar_k
   5. Set shadow price: gamma_t = (delta / (1 - delta)) * (1 / (T - t + 1))
   6. For each candidate subset F ⊆ [n] with |F| <= c:
         a. Compute active immediate risk: R_k0,k*(F | s^(t); H)
         b. Compute marginal graduation dividend: Delta L_grad(F; H)
         c. Compute online index:
               Q_t(F) = lambda * R_k0,k*(F) - gamma_t * Delta L_grad(F; H)
   7. Select optimal panel:
         F_t^* = argmin_{|F| <= c} Q_t(F)
   8. Reveal true features x_{F_t^*} to H; H chooses action y_hat_t and incurs regret
   9. Update exposure count: m_j <- m_j + 1 for all j in F_t^*
   10. Contract beliefs:
          H_kj <- U_kj + psi(m_j) * (H_kj^(0) - U_kj) for all (k, j) in [K] x F_t^*
End
```

##### **Computational Complexity:**
* At each round, candidate panels of size $c$ number $\binom{n}{c}$. For $c \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, this is $O(n^c)$.
* For each panel, evaluating $R_{k_0 k^*}$ takes $O(1)$ 1D Gauss-Hermite quadrature evaluations.
* Evaluating $\Delta \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}$ requires recomputing each margin's $F^*_{k\ell}$ (an argmax over $\binom{n}{c}$ panels) and its correlation: $O(K^2 n^c \cdot c^2)$ per candidate panel.
* Total per-round decision time is therefore $O(K^2 c^2 n^{2c})$ naively (polynomial for fixed $c$; the earlier $O(K^2 n^{c+1})$ omitted the inner argmax). Timing claims are untested.

### 6. The Autonomous Mode-Switching Mechanism

The Online Pedagogical Index $Q_t(F)$ autonomously navigates the trade-off between **Immediate Safety** and **Pedagogical Progress**:

```
                              Incoming Patient Presentation s^(t)
                                              │
                     ┌────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┐
                     ▼                                                 ▼
          Dangerous Misalignment                              Safe / Teachable Case
       p_k0,k*^T mu(s) * d_k0,k*^T mu(s) <= 0             p_k0,k*^T mu(s) * d_k0,k*^T mu(s) >> 0
                     │                                                 │
                     ▼                                                 ▼
          [DEFENSIVE SHIELDING]                             [ACTIVE DRILLING]
         lambda * R dominates objective                    R ≈ 0 for all candidate panels
                     │                                                 │
                     ▼                                                 ▼
       Select F_t to maximize local                       Select F_t to maximize marginal
      correlation rho_k0,k*(F_t) ≈ 1                       graduation dividend Delta L_grad
      (Prevents immediate mistake)                       (Debiases uncalibrated margins)
```

1. **Defensive Shielding Mode (High-Stakes Misalignment):**
   When symptoms present an instance where the human's prior momentum favors the wrong contender ($\mu_V \cdot \mu_D \le 0$) with high stakes ($|\mu_D| \gg 0$), immediate risk $\lambda R(F)$ spikes.
   The algorithm selects $F_t$ that minimizes immediate error, acting as a **protective copilot** to shield the human from catastrophic clinical harm.
2. **Active Drilling Mode (Safe Teachable Moments):**
   When symptoms naturally predispose $\mathcal{H}$ to the correct action ($\mu_V \cdot \mu_D \gg 0$), the risk term evaluates to $R \approx 0$ for all panels.
   The objective reduces strictly to maximizing $\Delta \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(F)$. The algorithm exploits the safe instance to drill high-salience distractors or uncalibrated margins required for graduation, acting as an **active teacher**.

### 7. Competitive Analysis & The Price of Real-Time Information

**Open question (formerly "Asymptotic Optimality Under Dominant Graduation Stakes" — retracted 2026-11-06).**
Is $\lim_{\lambda(1-\delta)/\delta \to 0} \mathcal{L}(\pi_{\text{M-AOP}}) / \mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{symptom}}) = 1$?

The previous proof is invalid. As $\lambda \to 0$, M-AOP becomes greedy maximization of the one-step dividend $\Delta \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}$, and it claimed greedy reaches the optimal allocation $m^*$ "by submodularity". The verified note [[4. Curriculum Allocation]] shows the opposite already for $K = 2$: $\rho(F^*(p(m)), p(m))$ is discontinuous in $m$ (salience switches), so the allocation problem "can't be reduced to knapsack or greedy". Concretely (e.g. $B = 0$), a feature outside the human's chosen panel can need several exposures before it becomes salient and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}$ moves at all, so its one-step dividend is $0$ (or negative, if extinguishing a distraction first hands salience to a wrong-signed feature) and greedy never starts it. So there is no reason to expect the ratio to tend to 1; a counterexample (or a proof under extra conditions) is open.
