# <G> - 5. Core Tradeoffs & Fundamental Theorems

This note formalizes the **core tradeoffs, non-monotonicities, and named theorems** that distinguish the decision-theoretic pedagogical framework from prior continuous prediction literature (e.g., Noti et al., EC 2025; Guan et al., 2026).

---

### 1. Structural Comparison: Continuous MSE vs. Discrete Pedagogical Regret

| Property | Continuous Prediction (Noti et al. 2025) | Pedagogical Assistance (This Work) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Loss Metric** | Quadratic MSE: $(y - \hat{y})^2$ | Discrete Decision Regret: $|d^\top x| \mathbb{1}[\text{wrong action}]$ |
| **Monotonicity** | Strictly Monotone: Any parameter update decreases MSE. | **Non-Monotone (The Kingmaker Trap)**: Partially teaching a feature can *increase* post-graduation regret! |
| **Policy Stationarity** | Stationary policies are globally optimal ($A_t = A^*$). | Stationary policies strictly fail; optimal policy undergoes **Vygotsky Phase Transition** from shielding to fading. |
| **Feature Regimes** | Informativeness vs. Divergence | **The Dangerous Medicine Trade-off**: Misunderstood ($p_j d_j < 0$) vs. Informative ($p_j d_j > 0$) vs. Distraction ($d_j = 0$). |
| **Role of Assistance** | Static filter (permanent crutch). | Dynamic Scaffolding ($\alpha_t \in [0, 1]$) with a formal **Price of Helpfulness**. |

---

### 2. Theorem 1: The "Kingmaker" & Crowding-Out Trap (Non-Monotonicity of Learning)

In continuous MSE, teaching a feature always strictly improves performance. Under autonomous test selection at graduation ($F^* = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} p_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_F$), this monotonicity breaks down catastrophically.

**Theorem 1 (The Non-Monotonicity of Partial Education):**
There exist configurations where partially de-biasing a feature strictly increases post-graduation regret:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(H^{(t+1)}\big) > \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(H^{(t)}\big) $$

**Proof by Construction (Crowding-Out):**
Consider budget $c = 1$ and two features $A, B$:
* Ground truth: Feature $B$ is highly informative ($d_B = 5$), while Feature $A$ is moderate ($d_A = 2$).
* Initial beliefs: $p_A^{(0)} = 0.5$ and $p_B^{(0)} = 4.0$.
* At baseline: The student's perceived salience is $(p_B^{(0)})^2 = 16 > (p_A^{(0)})^2 = 0.25$. The student autonomously selects $F^* = \{B\}$, achieving high correlation $\rho \approx 1$.
* Now suppose $\mathcal{A}$ partially teaches Feature $A$, moving its belief to $p_A^{(t)} = 4.5$, while leaving $B$ unchanged ($p_B^{(t)} = 4.0$).
* At graduation: The student ranks $(p_A^{(t)})^2 = 20.25 > (p_B^{(t)})^2 = 16$.
* The student **autonomously selects Feature $A$, crowding out the objectively superior Feature $B$**!
* Unassisted post-graduation regret increases strictly: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(H^{(t+1)}) > \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(H^{(0)})$. $\blacksquare$

**Pedagogical Implication:** A tutor cannot teach features greedily. To safely teach a secondary feature, the tutor must teach features in **coordinated bundles** to avoid crowning a "Kingmaker" that crowds out superior attributes.

### 3. Theorem 2: The Dangerous Medicine Trade-off

With discrete actions, features divide into two conflicting pedagogical classes:
1. **Informative Features ($p_j d_j > 0$):** The student already understands the direction of effect. Safe to show during training ($\ell_{\text{train}} = 0$), but diminishing debiasing returns.
2. **Misunderstood Features ($p_j d_j < 0$):** The student believes the feature favors the opposite action. Highly dangerous to show without scaffolding ($\ell_{\text{train}} = |d^\top x|$), but debiasing this feature flips entire decision boundaries.

**Theorem 2 (The Dangerous Medicine Principle):**
When training mistakes carry cost ($\lambda > 0$), the optimal policy exhibits a **bimodal separation**:
* On low-stakes cases ($|d^\top x|$ small), the AI prescribes **"Dangerous Medicine"**: deliberately exposing misunderstood features to force sign flips ($p_j d_j \to +$).
* On high-stakes cases ($|d^\top x|$ large), the AI retreats to **"Comfort Food"**: showing informative features or asserting directive control ($\alpha_t = 1$) to shield the system from immediate loss.

### 4. Theorem 3: The Price of Helpfulness (The Fundamental Pareto Frontier)

**Theorem 3 (The Sycophancy / Crutch Trap):**
Let $\Pi$ be the space of all tutoring policies. Define cumulative training loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{train}}(\pi) = \sum_{t=1}^T \mathbb{E}[\ell_{\text{train}}]$ and graduation regret $\mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(\pi)$.
1. **The Greedy Limit:** The policy $\pi_{\text{helpful}}$ that greedily minimizes training regret at every step ($\alpha_t \equiv 1$) achieves:
   $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{train}}(\pi_{\text{helpful}}) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(\pi_{\text{helpful}}) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(H^{(0)}\big) = \mathcal{O}(1) $$
2. **The Pareto Lower Bound:** For any policy $\pi$, post-graduation regret satisfies the lower bound:
   $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(\pi) \;\ge\; \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(H^{(0)}\big) \;-\; \Psi\left(\sum_{t=1}^T (1 - \alpha_t) C \right) $$
   where $\Psi(\cdot)$ is a strictly concave information-capacity function.

* **Significance:** You cannot produce an expert human without subjecting them to cognitive effort. Any reduction in graduation regret requires a non-zero cumulative allocation of **Socratic friction** ($\sum (1 - \alpha_t) > 0$).

### 5. Theorem 4: Zero-Regret Teachable Moments (The Margin Cliff Effect)

**Theorem 4 (Free Debiasing on High-Confidence Cases):**
Whenever observable symptoms $s^{(t)} = Bx^{(t)}$ provide strong directional momentum ($|p^\top \mu(s)| \gg 0$) such that:
$$ \operatorname{sign}\big(p^\top \mu(s)\big) = \operatorname{sign}\big(d^\top x\big) \quad \text{and} \quad |p^\top \mu(s)| > \max_{j} |p_j (x_j - \mu_j(s))| $$
The AI can set $\alpha_t = 0$ and expose **any misunderstood feature $j$**, achieving:
$$ \ell_{\text{train}} = 0 \quad \text{(Zero Training Regret)} \quad \text{and} \quad g_{t, j} = 1 \quad \text{(Maximum Debiasing)} $$

* **Intuition:** When a case's baseline presentation ($s$) is overwhelmingly clear, the student's final decision will be correct regardless of any subtle errors on individual features. The AI can exploit these **"bulletproof cases"** to teach complex, misunderstood factors with **zero risk of mistake**.

---

### 6. Theorem 5: Breakdown of Stationarity & Vygotsky Phase Transition

Noti et al. proved that under feature independence and MSE, stationary policies ($A_t = A^*$ forever) are globally optimal.

**Theorem 5 (Strict Sub-optimality of Stationary Scaffolding):**
Under graduation regret with $\lambda > 0$:
1. Any stationary policy $\pi_{\text{stat}}$ that keeps $\alpha_t = \bar{\alpha}$ constant for all $t$ suffers an asymptotic competitive gap:
   $$ \lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{\mathcal{L}(\pi_{\text{stat}})}{\mathcal{L}(\pi^*)} > 1 $$
2. The optimal policy $\pi^*$ strictly requires a **dynamic three-phase transition**:
   * **Phase I (Protection / Shielding):** $\alpha_t \approx 1$ on dangerous cases while identifying $H^{(0)}$.
   * **Phase II (Selective Teachable Moments):** $\alpha_t$ switches dynamically based on the zero-regret boundary.
   * **Phase III (Vygotsky Fading):** $\alpha_t \to 0$ monotonically as $t \to T$ to ensure the human's mental model is calibrated for autonomous deployment.
