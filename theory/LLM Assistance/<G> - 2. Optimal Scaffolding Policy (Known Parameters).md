# <G> - 2. Optimal Scaffolding Policy (Known Parameters)

This note solves for the optimal dynamic scaffolding policy $\pi^* = (M_1^*, \dots, M_T^*)$ in closed form when the algorithm $\mathcal{A}$ has full knowledge of ground truth utilities $U$, current human beliefs $H^{(t)}$, intrinsic plasticity $\eta$, and attention capacity budget $c$.

*(Note: User preference alignment and style adaptation are decoupled into [[<G> - 6. Latent Preference Alignment & Online Learning]].)*

---

### 1. The Pure Dynamic Objective

The algorithm $\mathcal{A}$ chooses the sequence of advice tuples $M_t = (\alpha_t, a_t, \hat{k}_t)$ to minimize:
$$ \mathcal{L}(\pi) := \sum_{t=1}^T \lambda \mathbb{E}\big[\ell_{\text{train}}(x^{(t)}, \hat{y}_t(M_t))\big] \;+\; \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(H^{(T)}\big) $$
subject to the attention capacity constraint:
$$ a_t \in [0, 1]^n, \qquad \sum_{j=1}^n a_{t, j} \le c $$
where:
* $\lambda \in [0, 1]$ parameterizes the cost of mistakes during training.
* $c \in (0, n]$ is the maximum continuous attention budget (generalizing $|F| \le c$ from the discrete setting).
* $\delta \in (0, 1)$ is the post-graduation discount factor.
* $\hat{y}_t(M_t) = \arg\max_{k \in [K]} \hat{u}_k(s^{(t)}, M_t; H^{(t)})$.
* From [[<G> - 3. Multi-Action Graduation Regret.md]], expected graduation regret under belief matrix $H$ is:
  $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(H) = \sum_{1 \le k < \ell \le K} P(s \in \mathcal{S}_{k\ell}) \sqrt{\frac{d_{k\ell}^\top \Sigma d_{k\ell}}{2\pi}} \Big(1 - \rho_{k\ell}\big(F_{k\ell}^*, p_{k\ell}\big)\Big) $$
  where $F_{k\ell}^* = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} (p_{k\ell})_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} (p_{k\ell})_F$.

---

### 2. The Dynamic Shadow Price: LLM Preceptor Index $\nu_j(t)$

By Bellman's principle, the marginal value of increasing active cognitive engagement on feature $j$ at round $t$ on post-graduation regret is the shadow price:
$$ \nu_j(t) := - \frac{\delta^{T-t}}{1-\delta} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}}{\partial G_j(t)} $$

Recall from [[<G> - 1. Problem Formulation & Two-Stage Architecture]] that:
$$ p_{k\ell, j}^{(T)} - d_{k\ell, j} = e^{-\tilde{\eta} G_j(T)} \big(p_{k\ell, j}^{(0)} - d_{k\ell, j}\big), \qquad \tilde{\eta} = -\ln(1 - \eta) $$
Applying the chain rule across active contender margins:
$$ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}}{\partial G_j(t)} = \sum_{1 \le k < \ell \le K} P(s \in \mathcal{S}_{k\ell}) \left( - \sqrt{\frac{d_{k\ell}^\top \Sigma d_{k\ell}}{2\pi}} \frac{\partial \rho_{k\ell}}{\partial p_{k\ell, j}} \right) \cdot \left( - \tilde{\eta} e^{-\tilde{\eta} G_j(t-1)} (p_{k\ell, j}^{(0)} - d_{k\ell, j}) \right) $$

**Definition (LLM Preceptor Index):**
The marginal pedagogical return of engaging feature $j$ at round $t$ is:
$$ \nu_j(t) = \frac{\delta^{T-t}}{1-\delta} \tilde{\eta} \, e^{-\tilde{\eta} G_j(t-1)} \cdot \mathcal{S}_j(H^{(t)}) $$
where $\mathcal{S}_j(H^{(t)})$ is the **multi-action feature salience**:
$$ \mathcal{S}_j(H^{(t)}) := \sum_{1 \le k < \ell \le K} P(s \in \mathcal{S}_{k\ell}) \sqrt{\frac{d_{k\ell}^\top \Sigma d_{k\ell}}{2\pi}} \left( \frac{\partial \rho_{k\ell}}{\partial p_{k\ell, j}} \right) \big( p_{k\ell, j}^{(t)} - d_{k\ell, j} \big) $$

Notice that:
* If feature $j$ is fully de-biased ($p_j \approx d_j$), $\mathcal{S}_j \to 0 \implies \nu_j(t) \to 0$.
* If feature $j$ is heavily misunderstood ($p_j d_j < 0$), salience $\mathcal{S}_j$ is large.
* The discount factor $\delta^{T-t}$ reflects **temporal proximity to graduation**: as $t \to T$, the shadow price peaks because there are fewer remaining rounds to correct misconceptions before the student must operate solo.

### 3. Step 1: Optimal Attention Allocation $a_t^*$ (Water-Filling)

At round $t$, with state $x^{(t)}$ and observation $s^{(t)}$, the single-round Lagrangian decomposes into:
$$ \min_{\alpha_t \in [0, 1], \; a_t \in [0, 1]^n} \left\{ \lambda \ell_{\text{train}}\big(x^{(t)}, \alpha_t, a_t\big) \;-\; (1 - \alpha_t) \sum_{j=1}^n a_{t, j} \nu_j(t) \right\} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \sum_{j=1}^n a_{t, j} \le c $$

Notice that for any fixed scaffolding level $\alpha_t \in [0, 1)$, the pedagogical return $(1 - \alpha_t) \sum_{j=1}^n a_{t, j} \nu_j(t)$ is **linear** in the attention weights $a_t$.

**Theorem 1 (Optimal Saliency Allocation via Water-Filling):**
For any given $\alpha_t < 1$, the attention allocation $a_t^*$ that maximizes the pedagogical return under budget $c$ is a **greedy water-filling policy** on the sorted Preceptor indices $\nu_{(1)}(t) \ge \nu_{(2)}(t) \ge \dots \ge \nu_{(n)}(t)$:
1. Let $j_1, \dots, j_n$ sort the features in descending order of shadow price: $\nu_{j_1}(t) \ge \nu_{j_2}(t) \ge \dots \ge \nu_{j_n}(t)$.
2. Allocate full attention $a_{t, j}^* = 1$ to the top features until the continuous budget $c$ is exhausted:
   $$ a_{t, j_r}^* = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } r \le \lfloor c \rfloor \\ c - \lfloor c \rfloor & \text{if } r = \lfloor c \rfloor + 1 \\ 0 & \text{if } r > \lfloor c \rfloor + 1 \end{cases} $$
3. When $c \le 1$ (single-focus guidance), $a_t^*$ concentrates the entire budget on the single highest-salience feature:
   $$ j^* = \arg\max_{j \in [n]} \nu_j(t), \qquad a_{t, j^*}^* = c, \quad a_{t, j \ne j^*}^* = 0 $$

### 4. Step 2: Optimal Scaffolding Degree $\alpha_t^*$ (The Socratic Switch)

With optimal attention $a_t^*$ determined, we now solve for the optimal scaffolding degree $\alpha_t^* \in [0, 1]$.

Let the total pedagogical rate of the case under optimal attention be:
$$ R(t) := \sum_{j=1}^n a_{t, j}^* \nu_j(t) $$
The objective reduces to a 1-dimensional decision:
$$ \min_{\alpha_t \in [0, 1]} \Phi(\alpha_t) := \lambda \ell_{\text{train}}\big(x^{(t)}, \alpha_t, a_t^*\big) \;-\; (1 - \alpha_t) R(t) $$

#### Regime Analysis: Safe vs. Teachable Cases
Let $y^* = \arg\max_k u_k^\top x^{(t)}$ be the ground-truth optimal action, and let:
$$ \hat{y}_{\text{soc}} = \arg\max_k \hat{u}_{k, \text{soc}}\big(s^{(t)}, a_t^*; H^{(t)}\big) $$
be the action the human would take if left entirely Socratic ($\alpha_t = 0$).

**Case 1: The Inherently Safe Case ($\hat{y}_{\text{soc}} = y^*$)**
* If the human would already select the correct action autonomously, $\ell_{\text{train}}(x^{(t)}, 0, a_t^*) = 0$.
* Setting $\alpha_t = 0$ achieves zero training loss while maximizing active engagement $1 - \alpha_t = 1$.
* **Optimal Action:** Set $\alpha_t^* = 0$ (Pure Socratic).

**Case 2: The Critical Teachable Case ($\hat{y}_{\text{soc}} \ne y^*$)**
* Here, unassisted Socratic reasoning leads the student to make an error of magnitude:
  $$ \Delta_{\text{reg}} := u_{y^*}^\top x^{(t)} - u_{\hat{y}_{\text{soc}}}^\top x^{(t)} > 0 $$
* There is a direct tension between safety and learning:
  * **Option Directive ($\alpha_t \to 1$):** $\ell_{\text{train}} = 0$, but learning return $= 0$. Total cost $= 0$.
  * **Option Socratic ($\alpha_t \to 0$):** $\ell_{\text{train}} = \Delta_{\text{reg}}$, learning return $= R(t)$. Total cost $= \lambda \Delta_{\text{reg}} - R(t)$.

**Theorem 2 (The Socratic Switching Policy):**
For any realized instance $(x^{(t)}, s^{(t)})$, the optimal scaffolding level $\alpha_t^*$ is given by the threshold rule:
$$ \alpha_t^* = \begin{cases} 0 \quad (\text{Socratic Mode: Let them learn}) & \text{if } \hat{y}_{\text{soc}} = y^* \;\;\text{OR}\;\; \lambda \Delta_{\text{reg}} \le R(t) \\ 1 \quad (\text{Directive Mode: Shield from disaster}) & \text{if } \hat{y}_{\text{soc}} \ne y^* \;\;\text{AND}\;\; \lambda \Delta_{\text{reg}} > R(t) \end{cases} $$

**Intuitive Reading:**
The assistant acts as an **adaptive shield**:
* It allows the student to struggle and make mistakes on benign/moderate cases where the learning gain exceeds the penalty ($\lambda \Delta_{\text{reg}} \le R(t)$).
* It steps in with a direct authoritative override ($\alpha_t = 1$) only when the training mistake would be catastrophic ($\lambda \Delta_{\text{reg}} > R(t)$).

---

### 5. The Vygotsky Fading Trajectory Theorem

**Theorem 3 (Systematic Fading as Graduation Approaches):**
Assume training mistakes carry positive cost $\lambda > 0$. As round $t \to T$:
1. The shadow price $\nu_j(t) = \frac{\delta^{T-t}}{1-\delta} \tilde{\eta} e^{-\tilde{\eta} G_j(t-1)} \mathcal{S}_j$ increases monotonically toward graduation:
   $$ \frac{\partial \nu_j(t)}{\partial t} > 0 \quad \text{for all } \delta \in (0, 1) $$
2. Consequently, the critical threshold for Socratic mode $\frac{R(t)}{\lambda}$ strictly increases with $t$.
3. The optimal policy exhibits **Vygotsky Scaffolding Fading**:
   $$ \mathbb{E}[\alpha_t^*] \quad \text{is non-increasing in } t $$
   The assistant starts more directive in early rounds ($t \approx 1$) to prevent large initial losses, and systematically fades its scaffolding toward pure Socratic guidance ($\alpha_t^* \to 0$) as graduation approaches.

---

### 6. The "Helpful AI Trap" Corollary

**Corollary 1 (Why Greedy Helpfulness Fails):**
Consider an assistant that greedily minimizes immediate error at every step ($\lambda \gg 0, \delta = 0$).
1. The greedy assistant sets $\alpha_t = 1$ on all rounds where $\hat{y}_{\text{soc}} \ne y^*$.
2. As a consequence, active engagement $G_j(T) = \sum (1 - \alpha_\tau) a_{\tau, j} \approx 0$.
3. Human beliefs remain frozen at initial bias: $H^{(T)} \approx H^{(0)}$.
4. Upon graduation ($t > T$), unassisted graduation regret is maximal:
   $$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(H^{(T)}\big) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(H^{(0)}\big) = \mathcal{O}(1) $$
   This analytically proves that standard "helpful" assistants act as a cognitive crutch that actively prevents human skill acquisition.
