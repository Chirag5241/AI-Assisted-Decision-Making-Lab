We first look at the feature selection rule by $\mathcal{H}$ and find a closed form expression for it.

Recall that during the graduation phase, the human tests to run which maximize her perceived value of information:
$$ F_{\mathcal{H}}^*(s; H^{(t)}) = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} \mathbb{E}_{x_F \mid s} \left[ \max_{k \in [K]} \hat{u}_k(F, x_F, s; H^{(t)}) \right] $$
In the two action case, we see that for a subset $F \subseteq [n]$ with $|F| \leq c$, the perceived difference in value between action 1 and 2 can be expressed as:
$$ 
\begin{align}
V(F, x_F, s; p) &= \sum_{j \in F} p_j x_j + \sum_{j \not\in F}p_j \mu_j(s)\\
&=\sum_{j \in [n]}p_j \mu_j(s) + \sum_{j \in F} p_j (x_j - \mu_j(s))\\
&= p^T\mu(s) + p_F^T(x_F - \mu_F(s))

\end{align}
$$
Note here that subscripting with set $F$ means the vector with only entries for indices in $F$.

**Claim 1:** The difference in value of the two actions $V(F) := V(F, x_F, s; p)$ for a subset $F \subseteq [n]$ can be expressed as normal distribution: $V(F) \sim \mathcal{N}(p^T \mu(s), \; p_F^T \; \Gamma_{FF} \; p_F)$  where $\Gamma_{FF}$ is only the rows and columns of $\Gamma = \Sigma_{x|s} = \operatorname{Var}[x|s]$ with indices in $F$, and $p := p^{(t)} = h_1^{(t)} - h_2^{(t)}$ is the vector of perceived differences.

**Proof:** 
Since $x | s \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu(s), \Gamma)$, then $x_F | s \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_F(s), \Gamma_{FF})$. Also $\mu_F(s) | s = \mu_F(s)$ is a constant, we then get that $x_F - \mu_F(s) | s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Gamma_{FF})$. Then $p_F^T(x_F - \mu_F(s)) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, p_F^T \; \Gamma_{FF} \; p_F)$ and $p^T \mu(s)$ is a constant, thus $p^T \mu(s) + p_F^T (x_F - \mu_F(s)) = V(F) | s \sim \mathcal{N}(p^T \mu(s), \; p_F^T \; \Gamma_{FF} \; p_F)$. 

We can see that $\mathcal{H}$ takes action 1 after seeing the features of $F$ whenever $V(F) > 0$. 
##### Value of Information:
The perceived value of information for seeing the set of features $F$, is how much more information $x_F$ provides, as opposed to not having seen any test results. We express this as: 
$$ \operatorname{VoI}(F | s) := \mathbb{E}_{x_F | s}\left[\max\{\hat{u}_1(F), \hat{u}_2(F)\} \right] - \max\{ \mathbb{E}_{x | s} \left[ \hat{u}_1(\emptyset)], \mathbb{E}[\hat{u}_2(\emptyset)\right]\}$$
where $\hat{u}_j(F) = \hat{u}_j(F, x_F, s; H^{(t)})$. 

**Note:** Finding an $F$ maximizing $\operatorname{VoI}(F|s)$ is equivalent of finding $F_{\mathcal{H}}^*(s; H^{(t)})$, since finding $F$ maximizing $\operatorname{VoI}(F|s)$, maximizes $\mathbb{E}_{x_F | s}\left[\max\{\hat{u}_1(F), \hat{u}_2(F)\} \right]$ (since the second term is independent of $F$), which is exactly $F_{\mathcal{H}}^*$ is the argmax of.

**Claim 2:** We can express the value of information for features $F$ as:
$$ \operatorname{VoI}(F | s) = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{E}[|V(F)|] - |p^T \mu(s)|)$$

**Proof:**
Using the fact that $\max\{a, b\} = \frac{1}{2}(a + b) + \frac{1}{2} |a - b|$, we can rewrite:
$$ 
\begin{align}
\mathbb{E}_{x_F | s}\left[\max\{\hat{u}_1(F), \hat{u}_2(F)\} \right] &= \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{x_F|s}\left[ \hat{u}_1(F) + \hat{u}_2(F)\right] + \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{x_F | s} \left[|V(F)|\right]\\
&= \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{x_F | s} [(h_1^{(t)} + h_2^{(t)})^T \mathbb{E}[x | x_F, s]] + \frac{1}{2} \mathbb{E}_{x_F | s}[|V(F)|]\\
&= \frac{1}{2}(h_1^{(t)} + h_2^{(t)})^T \mathbb{E}_{x_F|s}[\mathbb{E}[x | x_F, s]] + \frac{1}{2} \mathbb{E}_{x_F | s}[|V(F)|]\\
&= \frac{1}{2}(h_1^{(t)} + h_2^{(t)})^T \mu(s) + \frac{1}{2} \mathbb{E}_{x_F | s}[|V(F)|]
\end{align}
$$

We also know that $\mathbb{E}[\hat{u}_j(\emptyset)] = h_j^T \mu(s)$. Using this and the fact about maximum of two elements:
$$ 
\begin{align}
\max\{ \mathbb{E}_{x | s} \left[ \hat{u}_1(\emptyset)], \mathbb{E}[\hat{u}_2(\emptyset)\right]\} &= \max\{(h_1^{(t)})^T \mu(s), (h_2^{(t)})^T \mu(s)\}\\
&= \frac{1}{2}(h_1^{(t)} + h_2^{(t)})^T \mu(s) + \frac{1}{2} |p^T \mu(s)|
\end{align}
$$

Thus we can rewrite the perceived value of information formula as:
$$ \operatorname{VoI}(F | s) = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{E}[|V(F)|] - |p^T \mu(s)|)$$

**Fact 1:** For Gaussian variable $Z \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, we can express $\mathbb{E}[|Z|] = 2\sigma \, \phi\left(\frac{\mu}{\sigma}\right) + \mu \left( 2\Phi\left(\frac{\mu}{\sigma}\right) - 1 \right)$
where $\phi(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-z^2/2}$ is the standard normal PDF and $\Phi(z)$ is the standard normal CDF.
Furthermore, for any fixed $\mu \in \mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{E}[|Z|]$ is strictly increasing in $\sigma > 0$.

**Theorem:** The subset of features $F$ maximizing $\operatorname{VoI}(F | s)$ is $F^* = \arg\max_{F: |F| \leq c} \{p_F^T \; \Gamma_{FF} \; p_F \}$. 

**Proof:**
By Claim 2: $\operatorname{VoI}(F | s) = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{E}[|V(F)|] - |p^T \mu(s)|)$, which by Fact 1 is strictly increasing in $\sigma[V(F)] = \sqrt{p_F^T \; \Gamma_{FF} \; p_F}$ , since $V(F) \sim \mathcal{N}(p^T \mu(s), \; p_F^T \; \Gamma_{FF} \; p_F)$ by Claim 1, and $p^T\mu(s)$ is a constant. Thus maximizing the value of information for $F$ subject to the constraint that $|F| \leq c$ is equivalent to maximizing $p_F^T \; \Gamma_{FF} \; p_F$ for $|F| \leq c$. 

**Implication:** 
1. This theorem implies that picking $F^*$ optimal subset is independent of observation: $s$, since $\Gamma_{FF}$ depends on $\Sigma_{x | s} =\Sigma - \Sigma B^\top (B \Sigma B^\top)^{-1} B \Sigma$.
2. For independent features $x_i$, $\Gamma_{FF} = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1^2, ..., \sigma_n^2)$ and thus maximizing $p_F^T \; \Gamma_{FF} \; p_F$ is equivalent to maximizing $\sum_{j \in F} \sigma_j^2 p_j^2$ . Thus for independent features, we can compute $\sigma_j^2 p_j^2$ for each $j$, and select the highest $c$ values.
3. Picking features solely based off $\sigma_j^2 p_j^2$ is completely blind to whether the features are informative, misunderstood, or distractors.
