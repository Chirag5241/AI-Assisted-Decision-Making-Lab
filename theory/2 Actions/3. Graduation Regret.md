We now derive the closed form of the graduation regret incurred by $\mathcal{H}$ after graduation ($t > T$) for the binary decision setting, $K = 2$.

For state $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ the ground truth utility difference between action 1 and 2 is: $D := (u_1 - u_2)^T x = d^T x$. So if $D > 0$ action 1 is optimal, and $D < 0$ when action 2 optimal.

The human observes $s = Bx$, and selects features $F \subseteq [n]$ with $|F| \leq c$ to maximize the value of information. Here, $x \mid s \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu(s), \Gamma)$ where $\mu(s) = \Sigma B^\top (B \Sigma B^\top)^{-1} s = \Sigma B^\top (B \Sigma B^\top)^{-1} B x$ where $\Sigma B^\top (B \Sigma B^\top)^{-1} B =:\Pi$ is a projection matrix, and $\Gamma = \Sigma - \Sigma B^\top (B \Sigma B^\top)^{-1} B \Sigma$. 

Let $E_F \in \mathbb{R}^{c \times n}$ be the coordinate selection matrix selecting features in $F$, so $x_F = E_F x$. We can then express the perceived advantage of action 1 over action 2 as:
$$
\begin{align}
V(F) &= \sum_{j \in F} p_j x_j + \sum_{j \not\in F} p_j \mu_j(s)\\
&= p^T \mu(s) + p_F^T (x_F - \mu_F(s))\\
&= p^T \Pi x + p_F^T E_F(I_n - \Pi)x\\
&= (\Pi^T p)^T x + ((I_n - \Pi^T) E_F^T p_F)^T x\\
&= \Big(\Pi^T p + (I_n - \Pi^T) E_F^T p_F\Big)^T x
\end{align}
$$
Thus we can write $V(F) = w_F^T x$ for $w_F := \Pi^T p + (I_n - \Pi^T) E_F^T p_F$.

Since $x \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma)$, variables $D := d^T x$ and $V(F) = w_F^T x$ are jointly Gaussian. 
Here, $\mathbb{E}[D] = d^T \mathbb{E}[x] = 0$ and similarly $\mathbb{E}[V(F)] = 0$. 
Also, $\sigma_D^2 := \operatorname{Var}[D] = \mathbb{E}[(d^T x)^2] = d^T \; \mathbb{E}[x x^T] d = d^T \; \Sigma \; d$ and
$$ 
\begin{align}
\sigma_V^2 &:= \operatorname{Var}[V(F)] = w_F^T \Sigma w_F\\
&= \Big( \Pi^\top p + (I_n - \Pi^\top) E_F^\top p_F \Big)^\top \Sigma \Big( \Pi^\top p + (I_n - \Pi^\top) E_F^\top p_F \Big)\\
&= p^\top (\Sigma - \Gamma) p \;+\; p_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_F
\end{align}
$$
We can compute the covariance between $D$ and $V(F)$ as:
$$ 
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Cov}(D, V(F)) &= \mathbb{E}\Big[(d^T x)(w_F^T x)\Big] = d^T \Sigma w_F\\
&= d^T \Sigma \Big( \Pi^\top p + (I_n - \Pi^\top) E_F^\top p_F \Big)\\
&= d^\top \Sigma \Pi^\top p \;+\; d^\top \Sigma (I_n - \Pi^\top) E_F^\top p_F\\
&= d^T (\Sigma - \Gamma) p + p_F^T \; \Gamma_{F, :} \, d
\end{align}
$$
where $\Gamma_{F, :}$ is the rows of $\Gamma$ indexed by $F$. 

The Pearson correlation coefficient of $D$ and $V(F)$ is thus: 
$$ \rho(F, p) := \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(D, V(F))}{\sigma_D \sigma_V} = \frac{d^\top (\Sigma - \Gamma) p + p_F^\top \Gamma_{F, :} \, d}{\sqrt{d^\top \Sigma d} \;\; \sqrt{p^\top (\Sigma - \Gamma) p + p_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_F}} $$

$\mathcal{H}$ picks the wrong action when $\operatorname{sgn}(V(F)) \neq \operatorname{sgn}(D)$, and the regret incurred is $|D| = |d^T x|$. The loss for state $x$, given the human's perceived difference vector $p$ can be expressed as:
$$ \ell_{\text{grad}}(x; p) = |d^T x| \cdot \mathbb{1}\left[(d^T x) \cdot V(F) < 0\right]$$

**Fact:** For jointly Gaussian variables $U \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ and $Y \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$, we have that:
$$\mathbb{E}\Big[|U| \cdot \mathbb{1}[UY < 0]\Big] = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(1 - \operatorname{Cov}(U, Y)\right)$$

Normalizing, $X := \frac{D}{\sigma_D}$ and $Y := \frac{V(F)}{\sigma_V}$, then $X, Y \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$. 
Then, $\operatorname{Cov}(X, Y) = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(D, V(F))}{\sigma_D \sigma_V} = \rho(F, p)$.
Since $\sigma_D, \sigma_V > 0$, we have that:
$$ \operatorname{sgn}(D) = \operatorname{sgn}(X), \qquad \operatorname{sgn}(V(F)) = \operatorname{sgn}(Y) \implies \mathbb{1}[D \cdot V(F) < 0] = \mathbb{1}[X \cdot Y < 0] $$
Also, the absolute magnitude is $|D| = \sigma_D |X| = \sqrt{d^\top \Sigma d} \, |X|$.

We can thus write:
$$ 
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(p) &= \mathbb{E}\big[\ell_{\text{grad}}(x; p)\big] = \mathbb{E}\big[|d^T x| \cdot \mathbb{1}\left[(d^T x) \cdot V(F) < 0\right]\big]\\
&= \sqrt{\frac{d^\top \Sigma d}{2\pi}} \cdot \big(1 - \rho(F, p)\big)
\end{align}
$$

**Theorem:** For state from $x \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma)$ with $\mathcal{H}$ observing $s = Bx$, and perceived difference vector $p$, the expected graduation regret incurred for features $F$ selected is:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(p) = \sqrt{\frac{d^\top \Sigma d}{2\pi}} \cdot \big(1 - \rho(F, p)\big) $$
where:
$$ \rho(F, p) = \frac{d^\top (\Sigma - \Gamma) p + p_F^\top \Gamma_{F, :} \, d}{\sqrt{d^\top \Sigma d} \;\; \sqrt{p^\top (\Sigma - \Gamma) p + p_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_F}} $$
