We extend the $K = 2$, $\lambda > 0$ problem so that graduation is not scheduled: the human starts with the model, and the model can go missing at any round. Here the human's learning is **paused** while the model is away, and the model scores every departure as if it were permanent (it omits any dependence on its own future return). [[<G> - 7. Continued Human Learning]] lifts the first assumption and [[<G> - 8. Returning Assistance]] lifts the second.

Notation is that of [[1. 2 Actions Reduction]], [[2. Test Selection]], [[3. Graduation Regret]]: $d = u_1 - u_2$, $p(m)$ with $p_j(m_j) = (1 - \psi(m_j)) d_j + \psi(m_j) p_j^{(0)}$, $\Gamma = \Sigma_{x \mid s}$, and
$$ L(m) := \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(p(m)\big) = \kappa_d \Big(1 - \rho\big(F^*(p(m)), p(m)\big)\Big), \qquad \kappa_d := \sqrt{\frac{d^\top \Sigma d}{2\pi}}, \qquad F^*(p) = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} p_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_F . $$

### 1. Model

**Availability.** $a_t \in \{1, 0\}$ says whether the model $\mathcal{A}$ is present in round $t$. The human begins with the model, $a_1 = 1$. Availability is a Markov chain:
$$ \mathbb{P}(a_{t+1} = 1 \mid a_t = 1) = q, \qquad \mathbb{P}(a_{t+1} = 1 \mid a_t = 0) = r, \qquad q, r \in [0, 1). $$
$\mathcal{A}$ knows $q$ and knows it is present in round $t$, but not whether it will be present in round $t+1$.

**A present round** ($a_t = 1$) is a training round of [[Problem Description]]: $\mathcal{A}$ sees $s^{(t)}$, shows $F_t$, the human acts, and $m \leftarrow m + e_{F_t}$ with $e_F := \sum_{j \in F} e_j$. Its regret is now called the **model assistance regret**. Given $s$, its expectation is (Claim 3 of [[<G> - 5. Dynamic Assistance (Lambda > 0)]])
$$ R(F \mid s; p) := \mathbb{E}_{x \mid s}\Big[ |d^\top x| \cdot \mathbb{1}\big[(d^\top x) \cdot V(F, x_F, s; p) < 0\big] \Big], \qquad \bar{R}(F; p) := \mathbb{E}_s\big[R(F \mid s; p)\big] = \kappa_d \big(1 - \rho(F, p)\big). $$
The last equality is [[3. Graduation Regret]] with $F$ held fixed: averaging over $s$ gives the unconditional Gaussian pair $(D, V(F))$.

**An absent round** ($a_t = 0$) is a graduation round: the human picks $F^*(p)$ (symptom-independent, [[2. Test Selection]]) and has expected regret $L(m)$. **Paused learning:** $m$ does not change.

**Omitting future assistance.** $\mathcal{A}$ plans as if $r = 0$: once it leaves, it never returns. Then the first absent round is a graduation at the random time
$$ \tau := \min\{t : a_{t+1} = 0\}, \qquad \mathbb{P}(\tau \ge t) = q^{t-1}, $$
and paused learning freezes the human at $p(m_\tau)$ forever, where $m_t$ is the count vector **after** round $t$.

**Objective.** The objective of [[Problem Description]] with $T$ replaced by $\tau$:
$$ \mathcal{L}(\pi) := \mathbb{E}\Big[ \lambda \sum_{t=1}^{\tau} \ell_{\text{reg}}(x^{(t)}, \hat{y}_t) \;+\; \sum_{t > \tau} \delta^{t - \tau} \, \ell_{\text{grad}}(x^{(t)}; p(m_\tau)) \Big]. $$
Conditioned on $\tau = T$ this is exactly the verified objective, $\lambda \sum_{t \le T} \mathbb{E}[\ell_{\text{reg}}] + \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} L(m_T)$.

### 2. Every round may be the last

**Proposition 1 (Hazard-weighted objective).** For any causal policy $\pi$,
$$ \mathcal{L}(\pi) = \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} q^{t-1} \, \mathbb{E}\Big[ \lambda \, R(F_t \mid s^{(t)}; p(m_{t-1})) \;+\; w \, L(m_t) \Big], \qquad w := \frac{(1-q)\,\delta}{1-\delta}. $$

**Proof.** $\tau$ is independent of the draws and of $\pi$ ($\mathcal{A}$'s choices do not change availability). Round $t$ is assisted iff $\tau \ge t$, which has probability $q^{t-1}$; its expected regret given $s^{(t)}$ and $m_{t-1}$ is $R(F_t \mid s^{(t)}; p(m_{t-1}))$. The tail after $\tau = t$ has expected value $\sum_{i \ge 1} \delta^i L(m_t) = \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} L(m_t)$, and $\mathbb{P}(\tau = t) = q^{t-1}(1-q)$. Summing both parts by Fubini (all terms are non-negative) gives the claim. $\blacksquare$

So every assisted round is charged twice: $\lambda R$ for the decision made with the model, and $w L$ for the human it would leave behind if the model vanished right after. Under a fixed $T$, only $L(m_T)$ is charged; here **every intermediate $L(m_t)$ is**. A curriculum that passes through bad intermediate states (e.g. it extinguishes a distraction before the best feature is qualified, so that a misunderstood feature becomes the most salient) is penalised for that, even if it ends well.

**Bellman equation.** The state is the count vector $m$ (and the current $s$). Let $\bar{J}(m) := \mathbb{E}_s[J(m, s)]$. Then
$$ J(m, s) = \min_{|F| \le c} \Big\{ \lambda R(F \mid s; p(m)) \;+\; w \, L(m + e_F) \;+\; q \, \bar{J}(m + e_F) \Big\}, $$
and $\mathcal{L}(\pi^*) = \bar{J}(0)$. Survival $q$ plays the role of a discount factor, so $\bar{J}$ is finite ($L, R \le 2\kappa_d$).

**Limits.**
1. $q = 0$ (model present for one round only): $J(m, s) = \min_F \{\lambda R(F \mid s; p(m)) + \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} L(m + e_F)\}$, a one-round trade-off.
2. $\lambda \to 0$: $\mathcal{L} \to w \sum_t q^{t-1} L(m_t)$. The model wants the **whole path** $L(m_1), L(m_2), \dots$ low, weighted by survival — an anytime curriculum, not the single allocation $m^*$ of [[4. Curriculum Allocation]].
3. Fixed departure ($\tau = T$ almost surely) recovers [[<G> - 5. Dynamic Assistance (Lambda > 0)]] (Symptom-Clairvoyant/online DP with terminal cost $\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}L$).

**Myopic index (heuristic).** Dropping $q \bar{J}$ gives the one-step rule
$$ F_t = \arg\min_{|F| \le c} \Big\{ \lambda R(F \mid s^{(t)}; p(m_{t-1})) + w \, L(m_{t-1} + e_F) \Big\}. $$
It is exact for $q = 0$. For $q > 0$ it is only a heuristic: a misunderstood feature can need several exposures before $L$ drops, so its one-step gain can be zero or negative (cf. [[4. Curriculum Allocation]], where greedy fails for the same reason).

### 3. Closed form in the independent case

Take $B = 0$ (no symptoms: $\mu(s) = 0$, $\Pi = 0$, $\Gamma = \Sigma$), $\Sigma = \operatorname{diag}(\Sigma_{11}, \dots, \Sigma_{nn})$, $c = 1$. Write $\sigma_j := \Sigma_{jj} d_j^2$ (true informativeness of $j$), so $d^\top \Sigma d = \sum_j \sigma_j$.

**Lemma 1.** For $F = \{j\}$,
$$ \rho(\{j\}, p) = \operatorname{sgn}(p_j d_j) \sqrt{\frac{\sigma_j}{d^\top \Sigma d}}, \qquad \bar{R}(\{j\}; p) = \kappa_d \Big(1 - \operatorname{sgn}(p_j d_j)\sqrt{\tfrac{\sigma_j}{d^\top \Sigma d}}\Big), $$
with $\operatorname{sgn}(0) = 0$ (a tie broken by a fair coin), and $L(m) = \bar{R}(\{j_m\}; p(m))$ for $j_m := \arg\max_j \Sigma_{jj} p_j(m_j)^2$.

**Proof.** With $\Pi = 0$, $\Gamma = \Sigma$: $\rho = \frac{p_j \Sigma_{jj} d_j}{\sqrt{d^\top \Sigma d}\, |p_j| \sqrt{\Sigma_{jj}}}$, which simplifies to the display. If $p_j = 0$ the human is indifferent on every $x$; a fair coin errs with probability $\tfrac12$, giving $\tfrac12 \mathbb{E}|D| = \kappa_d$. $\blacksquare$

So per-round costs take at most $2n + 1$ values: the model's choice matters only through **which** feature is read and **whether its sign is right**; the magnitude of $p_j$ matters only for salience (which feature the human picks alone). With no symptoms, $R$ does not depend on $s$ and the Bellman equation is a deterministic shortest path over counts:
$$ J(m) = \min_{j} \Big\{ \lambda \bar{R}(\{j\}; p(m)) + w \, L(m + e_j) + q \, J(m + e_j) \Big\}. $$

**Teaching costs now.** Showing a feature the human reads with the right sign costs $\kappa_d(1 - \sqrt{\sigma_j / d^\top \Sigma d}) < \kappa_d$; the smallest value, $L_{\min} := \kappa_d\big(1 - \sqrt{\sigma_{j^*}/d^\top \Sigma d}\big)$ with $j^* := \arg\max_j \sigma_j$, is also the smallest graduation loss reachable with $c = 1$. Showing a distraction costs $\kappa_d$, and showing a misunderstood feature costs $\kappa_d(1 + \sqrt{\sigma_j / d^\top \Sigma d}) > \kappa_d$. So every exposure spent on extinguishing or correcting pays $\lambda$ times a strictly larger regret than any right-signed feature would, and only the hazard weight $w$ (the graduation loss it removes) can pay for it.

### 4. What this note does not cover

* The human learns nothing while alone. If they keep learning from the features they choose, the departure cost $\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}L(m_\tau)$ is replaced by a solo-path value: [[<G> - 7. Continued Human Learning]].
* The model does return ($r > 0$). With paused learning this is an exact reweighting of the equation above (Corollary 1 of [[<G> - 8. Returning Assistance]]); with continued learning the model must re-plan from the updated human state.
