We analyze the binary decision setting ($K = 2$) when training decisions carry real-world cost ($\lambda > 0$). In this regime, the algorithm $\mathcal{A}$ must assist $\mathcal{H}$ on incoming cases in real time, balancing immediate decision accuracy against long-term debiasing for graduation.

---

### 1. Problem Formulation ($\lambda > 0$)

Recall that state $x \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma) \in \mathbb{R}^n$, observation $s = Bx \in \mathbb{R}^d$, and conditional distribution $x \mid s \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu(s), \Gamma)$, where $\mu(s) = \Pi x$ with $\Pi = \Sigma B^\top (B \Sigma B^\top)^{-1} B \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ and residual covariance $\Gamma = \Sigma - \Sigma B^\top (B \Sigma B^\top)^{-1} B \Sigma \succeq 0$.

The ground truth difference vector is $d := u_1 - u_2 \in \mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{0\}$. For any state $x$, the true utility difference between Action 1 and Action 2 is $D := d^\top x$, with Action 1 optimal iff $d^\top x \ge 0$.

At training round $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$:
1. Nature realizes latent state $x^{(t)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma)$ and observation $s^{(t)} = Bx^{(t)}$.
2. Algorithm $\mathcal{A}$ selects a feature subset $F_t \subseteq [n]$ with $|F_t| \le c$ to reveal to $\mathcal{H}$.
3. $\mathcal{H}$ observes $s^{(t)}$ and true features $x_{F_t}^{(t)}$, and imputes $\mu_j(s^{(t)})$ for unrevealed features $j \notin F_t$.
   The perceived value difference between Action 1 and Action 2 held by $\mathcal{H}$ on patient $t$ is:
   $$ V_t(F_t) := V(F_t, x_{F_t}^{(t)}, s^{(t)}; p^{(t)}) = p^{(t)\top} \mu(s^{(t)}) + p_{F_t}^{(t)\top} \big(x_{F_t}^{(t)} - \mu_{F_t}(s^{(t)})\big) $$
   where $p^{(t)} := h_1^{(t)} - h_2^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is the perceived difference vector at time $t$.
4. $\mathcal{H}$ selects action $\hat{y}_t = 1$ if $V_t(F_t) \ge 0$, and $\hat{y}_t = 2$ if $V_t(F_t) < 0$.
5. Decision regret suffered on round $t$ is:
   $$ \ell_{\text{train}}(x^{(t)}, F_t; p^{(t)}) = |d^\top x^{(t)}| \cdot \mathbb{1}\big[ (d^\top x^{(t)}) \cdot V_t(F_t) < 0 \big] $$
6. **Belief Update Dynamic:**
   For all revealed features $j \in F_t$, the cumulative exposure count increments $m_j(t) = m_j(t-1) + 1$, contracting perceived error:
   $$ p_j^{(t+1)} = (1 - \psi(m_j(t))) d_j + \psi(m_j(t)) p_j^{(0)} $$
   For unrevealed features $j \notin F_t$, beliefs remain frozen: $p_j^{(t+1)} = p_j^{(t)}$.

##### **The Dual Training-Graduation Objective:**
The objective of the algorithm $\mathcal{A}$ is to choose the sequence of feature reveals $\pi = (F_1, \dots, F_T)$ to minimize:
$$ \mathcal{L}(\pi) := \lambda \sum_{t=1}^T \ell_{\text{train}}(x^{(t)}, F_t; p^{(t)}) \;+\; \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(p(m(T))\big) $$
where $\lambda \in (0, 1]$ parameterizes the cost of training mistakes, $\delta \in (0, 1)$ is the post-graduation discount factor, and from [[3. Graduation Regret.md]]:
$$ \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(p) = \sqrt{\frac{d^\top \Sigma d}{2\pi}} \Big(1 - \rho\big(F^*(p), p\big)\Big) $$
with $F^*(p) = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} p_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_F$.

### 2. Why Symptoms $s$ Directly Impact Training Decisions

**Observation (The Graduation vs. Training Asymmetry):**
In [[2. Test Selection.md]], we proved that at graduation ($t > T$), $\mathcal{H}$'s autonomous test choice $F^* = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} p_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_F$ is completely independent of $s$. However, during training ($t \le T$), **symptoms $s^{(t)}$ directly determine the difficulty and safety of each case**:

The decision variable on round $t$ decomposes into:
$$ V_t(F_t) = \underbrace{p^{(t)\top} \mu(s^{(t)})}_{\text{Prior Momentum from } s^{(t)}} \;+\; \underbrace{p_{F_t}^{(t)\top} \big(x_{F_t}^{(t)} - \mu_{F_t}(s^{(t)})\big)}_{\text{Test Innovation from } F_t} $$
The observation $s^{(t)}$ provides a deterministic prior bias $p^{(t)\top} \mu(s^{(t)})$. This creates two fundamental regimes:

1. **The Safe Prior Regime ($|p^{(t)\top} \mu(s^{(t)})| \gg 0$ in agreement with $d^\top x^{(t)}$):**
   If the prior momentum strongly favors the true optimal action, $\operatorname{sgn}(V_t(F_t)) = \operatorname{sgn}(d^\top x^{(t)})$ holds for almost all test innovations. The human is already predisposed to choose correctly based on $s^{(t)}$ alone.
   * *Pedagogical Consequence:* On these instances, $\mathcal{A}$ can safely reveal misunderstood features or distractors to debias $\mathcal{H}$ for graduation at virtually zero risk of immediate error.
2. **The Danger / Misaligned Regime ($p^{(t)\top} \mu(s^{(t)}) \cdot (d^\top x^{(t)}) \le 0$):**
   If symptoms bias $\mathcal{H}$ toward the wrong action or leave $\mathcal{H}$ equivocal, an uncalibrated feature reveal will result in an immediate error with cost $|d^\top x^{(t)}|$.
   * *Pedagogical Consequence:* $\mathcal{A}$ must act defensively ("shielding") by revealing highly informative features that overpower the erroneous prior bias and guide $\mathcal{H}$ to the correct choice.

### 3. Teachable Moments & The Zero-Regret Set

**Definition (The Zero-Regret Set):**
For any state $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and observation $s = Bx$, given current belief vector $p$, the **zero-regret set** $Z(x, s; p)$ is the collection of feature subsets of size at most $c$ whose observation guarantees the correct decision on instance $x$:
$$ Z(x, s; p) := \left\{ F \subseteq [n], |F| \le c : (d^\top x) \cdot \Big( p^\top \mu(s) + p_F^\top \big(x_F - \mu_F(s)\big) \Big) > 0 \right\} $$

**Claim 1 (Free Teachable Moment):**
If $\mathcal{A}$ selects any feature set $F_t \in Z(x^{(t)}, s^{(t)}; p^{(t)})$, the immediate decision regret on round $t$ is identically zero:
$$ \ell_{\text{train}}(x^{(t)}, F_t; p^{(t)}) = 0 $$
while the cumulative exposure count increments for all $j \in F_t$, contracting the perceived error toward the ground truth:
$$ |p_j^{(t+1)} - d_j| = \psi(m_j(t)) |p_j^{(0)} - d_j| \quad \forall j \in F_t $$
(with $m_j(t)$ already counting round $t$, as in the update rule of §1).

**Claim 2 (Probability of Free Teaching under Residual Covariance):**
Given observation $s$, let $z = x - \mu(s) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Gamma)$. For any candidate feature set $F$, the true advantage $D = d^\top x = d^\top \mu(s) + d^\top z$ and perceived advantage $V(F) = p^\top \mu(s) + p_F^\top z_F$ are jointly Gaussian:
$$ \begin{pmatrix} D \\ V(F) \end{pmatrix} \;\Bigg|\; s \;\sim\; \mathcal{N}\left( \begin{pmatrix} d^\top \mu(s) \\ p^\top \mu(s) \end{pmatrix}, \; \begin{pmatrix} d^\top \Gamma d & p_F^\top \Gamma_{F, :} d \\ p_F^\top \Gamma_{F, :} d & p_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_F \end{pmatrix} \right) $$
The probability that $F$ belongs to the zero-regret set given $s$ is the orthant probability:
$$ \mathbb{P}\big(F \in Z(x, s; p) \mid s\big) = \Phi_2\left( \frac{d^\top \mu(s)}{\sqrt{d^\top \Gamma d}}, \; \frac{p^\top \mu(s)}{\sqrt{p_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_F}} ; \; \tilde{\rho}(F) \right) + \Phi_2\left( -\frac{d^\top \mu(s)}{\sqrt{d^\top \Gamma d}}, \; -\frac{p^\top \mu(s)}{\sqrt{p_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_F}} ; \; \tilde{\rho}(F) \right) $$
where $\Phi_2(\cdot, \cdot; \rho)$ is the standard bivariate normal CDF with correlation coefficient:
$$ \tilde{\rho}(F) := \frac{p_F^\top \Gamma_{F, :} d}{\sqrt{d^\top \Gamma d} \;\; \sqrt{p_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_F}} $$

*Implication:* Even when a feature $j$ is misunderstood ($p_j d_j < 0$), the probability that $\{j\} \in Z(x, s; p)$ is positive whenever the pair is non-degenerate ($|\tilde{\rho}| < 1$), and it approaches 1 when $p^\top \mu(s)$ and $d^\top \mu(s)$ agree in sign and are large relative to $\sigma_V$ and $\sigma_D$. The preceptor actively waits for these instances to debias misunderstood features at zero immediate risk.

### 4. Regime 1: The Full Oracle Benchmark

In the Full Oracle regime, $\mathcal{A}$ knows the entire sequence of realized latent states $x^{(1)}, \dots, x^{(T)}$ and observations $s^{(1)}, \dots, s^{(T)}$ before the training phase begins.

##### **Formulation as an Exact Dynamic Program:**
Because $x^{(t)}$ is known, the true advantage $D^{(t)} = d^\top x^{(t)}$ and the innovation vector $z^{(t)} = x^{(t)} - \mu(s^{(t)})$ are known numbers.
For any candidate feature set $F$ and current exposure vector $m = (m_1, \dots, m_n) \in \mathbb{N}^n$, the post-test perceived difference is deterministic:
$$ V_t(F; m) = p(m)^\top \mu(s^{(t)}) + p_F(m)^\top \big(x_F^{(t)} - \mu_F(s^{(t)})\big) $$
The immediate regret on round $t$ evaluates deterministically:
$$ g_t(m, F) := \lambda |d^\top x^{(t)}| \cdot \mathbb{1}\big[ (d^\top x^{(t)}) \cdot V_t(F; m) < 0 \big] \in \big\{ 0, \; \lambda |d^\top x^{(t)}| \big\} $$

The Oracle problem is a finite-horizon deterministic dynamic program:
* **State Space:** Exposure count vector $m(t) \in \mathbb{N}^n$ with $\sum_{j=1}^n m_j(t) \le c(t-1)$ (equality if every panel uses the full budget).
* **Action Space:** $F_t \subseteq [n]$ with $|F_t| \le c$.
* **State Transition:** $m(t+1) = m(t) + \sum_{j \in F_t} e_j$.
* **Terminal Cost at round $T+1$:**
  $$ J_{T+1}(m) = \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(p(m)\big) = \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \sqrt{\frac{d^\top \Sigma d}{2\pi}} \Big(1 - \rho\big(F^*(p(m)), p(m)\big)\Big) $$
* **Bellman Optimality Equation:**
  $$ J_t(m) = \min_{F \subseteq [n], |F| \le c} \Big\{ g_t(m, F) + J_{t+1}(m + e_F) \Big\} \quad \text{for } t = T, T-1, \dots, 1 $$
  where $e_F := \sum_{j \in F} e_j$.

##### **Structural Properties of the Oracle Solution:**
1. **Teachable Moment Exploitation:**  
   Every $F \in Z(x^{(t)}, s^{(t)}; p(m))$ has $g_t(m, F) = 0$, so among zero-cost panels the Oracle simply picks $\arg\min_{F \in Z} J_{t+1}(m + e_F)$. A zero-cost panel may contain misunderstood features or distractors: these are the rounds where they can be exposed for free.
2. **Critical Case Shielding:**  
   The Oracle picks some $F' \notin Z$ only if $\lambda |d^\top x^{(t)}| + J_{t+1}(m + e_{F'}) < \min_{F \in Z} J_{t+1}(m + e_F)$. Since $J_{t+1}$ varies by at most a bounded amount across panels, once $\lambda |d^\top x^{(t)}|$ exceeds that spread (and $Z \neq \emptyset$) the Oracle stays inside $Z$, shielding the human on high-stakes cases.
3. **Complexity:**  
   The reachable states satisfy $\sum_j m_j(t) \le c(t-1)$ and $m_j(t) \le t-1$; their number is at most $\binom{c(t-1) + n}{n}$. For small $n$ (e.g. $n \le 10$), this DP is solved exactly via backward induction. For larger $n$, it provides the ideal benchmark against which greedy and online heuristics are evaluated.

### 5. Regime 2: The Symptom-Based Clairvoyant Benchmark

In the Symptom Clairvoyant regime, $\mathcal{A}$ knows the sequence of presenting symptoms $s^{(1)}, \dots, s^{(T)}$ in advance (e.g., a pre-scheduled patient intake roster), but the latent state $x^{(t)}$ is not revealed until round $t$:
$$ x^{(t)} \mid s^{(t)} \sim \mathcal{N}\big(\mu(s^{(t)}), \; \Gamma\big) $$

##### **Expected Immediate Regret Given $s^{(t)}$:**
Unlike the Oracle, the Symptom Clairvoyant cannot guarantee zero loss on an individual patient because of residual uncertainty $\Gamma$.
For a chosen feature set $F$ and belief $p = p(m)$, the expected immediate regret on patient $t$ given symptoms $s^{(t)}$ is:
$$ R(F \mid s^{(t)}; p) := \mathbb{E}_{x \mid s^{(t)}}\Big[ |d^\top x| \cdot \mathbb{1}\big[(d^\top x) \cdot V(F, x_F, s^{(t)}; p) < 0\big] \Big] $$

**Claim 3 (Closed-Form Expected Regret under Non-Zero Mean):**
Let $\mu_D = d^\top \mu(s^{(t)})$, $\mu_V = p^\top \mu(s^{(t)})$, $\sigma_D = \sqrt{d^\top \Gamma d}$, $\sigma_V = \sqrt{p_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_F}$, and correlation $\tilde{\rho}(F) = \frac{p_F^\top \Gamma_{F, :} d}{\sigma_D \sigma_V}$.
Then $R(F \mid s^{(t)}; p)$ is given by the non-central bivariate Gaussian orthant integral:
$$ R(F \mid s^{(t)}; p) = \sigma_D \int_{-\infty}^\infty \phi(w) \cdot \Big| w + \frac{\mu_D}{\sigma_D} \Big| \cdot \Phi\left( -\operatorname{sgn}\Big(w + \frac{\mu_D}{\sigma_D}\Big) \cdot \frac{\mu_V / \sigma_V + \tilde{\rho}(F) w}{\sqrt{1 - \tilde{\rho}(F)^2}} \right) dw $$
(This needs $\sigma_V > 0$ and $|\tilde{\rho}| < 1$. If $\sigma_V = 0$, e.g. $F = \emptyset$, then $V = \mu_V$ is constant and $R = \mathbb{E}\big[|D|\,\mathbb{1}[D \mu_V < 0]\big]$, a one-sided folded-normal mean.)
When symptoms are uninformative ($s = 0$, so $\mu_D = \mu_V = 0$), this recovers the Fact of [[3. Graduation Regret]]:
$$ R(F \mid 0; p) = \frac{\sigma_D}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \big(1 - \tilde{\rho}(F)\big) $$

##### **The Symptom Clairvoyant Dynamic Program:**
The Symptom Clairvoyant solves the stochastic dynamic program with known non-stationary symptom sequence $s^{(1)}, \dots, s^{(T)}$:
$$ J_t(m) = \min_{F \subseteq [n], |F| \le c} \Big\{ \lambda R(F \mid s^{(t)}; p(m)) + J_{t+1}(m + e_F) \Big\} $$
subject to terminal condition:
$$ J_{T+1}(m) = \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}\big(p(m)\big) $$

##### **Cohort Scheduling Algorithm:**
Because $\mathcal{A}$ knows all $s^{(1)}, \dots, s^{(T)}$ upfront:
1. It partitions the training sequence into **High-Stakes Symptom Rounds** ($|\mu_D(s^{(t)})| \gg 0$) and **Equivocal Rounds** ($\mu_D(s^{(t)}) \approx 0$).
2. It reserves high-stakes rounds for safe feature sets with high correlation $\tilde{\rho}(F) \approx 1$.
3. It assigns uncalibrated distractor features to rounds where the symptom bias $\mu_V(s^{(t)}) \cdot \mu_D(s^{(t)}) \gg 0$ naturally suppresses $R(F \mid s^{(t)}; p) \approx 0$.

### 6. Regime 3: The Online Dynamic Assistant

In realistic deployment, $\mathcal{A}$ does not know future symptoms in advance. At each round $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$, $\mathcal{A}$ observes $s^{(t)}$ in real time, inspects current belief $p^{(t)} = p(m(t-1))$, and must select $F_t$ without foresight.

##### **The Real-Time Bellman Formulation:**
At round $t$, the optimal online choice satisfies:
$$ F_t^* = \arg\min_{|F| \le c} \left\{ \lambda R(F \mid s^{(t)}; p(m(t-1))) + \mathbb{E}_{s^{(t+1:T)}}\Big[ J_{t+1}(m(t-1) + e_F) \Big] \right\} $$
Evaluating the future expectation $\mathbb{E}_{s^{(t+1:T)}}[\dots]$ requires nested dynamic programming over all possible future Gaussian paths, which is computationally intractable for real-time triage.

##### **The Online Shield-vs-Teach Index Algorithm:**
To achieve real-time polynomial-time execution, we approximate the future continuation value by the marginal graduation dividend.

**Definition (Marginal Graduation Dividend):**
The marginal graduation dividend of revealing feature set $F$ given current exposure count $m$ is:
$$ \Delta \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(F; m) := \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(p(m)) - \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(p(m + e_F)) $$
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(p) = \sqrt{\frac{d^\top \Sigma d}{2\pi}} (1 - \rho(F^*(p), p))$.

We define the **Online Pedagogical Index**:
$$ Q_t(F \mid s^{(t)}; m) := \lambda \cdot R(F \mid s^{(t)}; p(m)) \;-\; \gamma_t \cdot \Delta \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(F; m) $$
where $\gamma_t := \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \cdot \frac{1}{T - t + 1}$ is the amortized shadow price of graduation learning.

*Status: heuristic.* $\gamma_t$ is not derived from the Bellman equation, and $\Delta \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(F; m)$ is a one-step quantity: it can be zero or negative for a misunderstood feature that needs several exposures (or for extinguishing a distraction that hands salience to a misunderstood feature), so ODP can under-teach exactly the features [[4. Curriculum Allocation]] shows greedy cannot handle.

```
Algorithm: Online Dynamic Preceptor (ODP) for K = 2
Input: Ground truth d, prior beliefs p^(0), budget c, discount delta, weight lambda, T
Initialize: m = (0, ..., 0) in N^n

For t = 1 to T:
   1. Observe incoming symptom vector s^(t)
   2. Compute conditional state mean mu(s^(t)) = Sigma B^T (B Sigma B^T)^{-1} s^(t)   (from s only; x^(t) is unseen)
   3. For each candidate subset F ⊆ [n] with |F| <= c:
         a. Compute expected immediate risk: R(F | s^(t); p(m)) via Claim 3
         b. Compute marginal graduation dividend: Delta L_grad(F; m)
         c. Compute online index:
               Q_t(F | s^(t); m) = lambda * R(F | s^(t); p(m)) - gamma_t * Delta L_grad(F; m)
   4. Select optimal test panel:
         F_t^* = argmin_{|F| <= c} Q_t(F | s^(t); m)
   5. Reveal true features x_{F_t^*} to H; observe prediction y_hat_t and incur loss
   6. Update exposure vector: m_j <- m_j + 1 for all j in F_t^*
   7. Contract beliefs: p_j(m_j) = (1 - psi(m_j)) d_j + psi(m_j) p_j^(0)
End
```

##### **The Mode-Switching Behavior of the Online Index:**
The Online Index automatically switches between two distinct behavioral modes based on symptoms $s^{(t)}$:

1. **Defensive Shielding Mode (High-Risk Symptom Presentation):**
   When $s^{(t)}$ presents a case where $|d^\top \mu(s^{(t)})|$ is large but the human's prior $p^\top \mu(s^{(t)})$ is misaligned, the immediate risk term $\lambda R(F \mid s^{(t)})$ dominates.
   The algorithm selects $F_t$ to minimize $R(F \mid s^{(t)})$, acting as a **protective copilot** to prevent a disastrous mistake today.
2. **Aggressive Teaching Mode (Safe / Teachable Symptom Presentation):**
   When $s^{(t)}$ is an instance where $p^\top \mu(s^{(t)})$ strongly aligns with $d^\top \mu(s^{(t)})$, the immediate risk is negligible ($R(F \mid s^{(t)}) \approx 0$ for all $F$).
   The objective reduces to maximizing $\Delta \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(F; m)$. The algorithm selects $F_t$ to drill the most critical uncalibrated distractor or misunderstood feature, acting as an **active teacher**.

### 7. Theoretical Comparison Across Regimes

The three regimes establish a rigorous performance hierarchy:

**Theorem (The Pedagogical Information Hierarchy):**
Let $\mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{oracle}})$, $\mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{symptom}})$, and $\mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{online}})$ denote the total cumulative loss under the optimal Oracle, Symptom Clairvoyant, and Online policies, respectively. For any $\lambda > 0$, training budget $T$, and initial belief $p^{(0)}$:
$$ \mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{oracle}}) \;\le\; \mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{symptom}}) \;\le\; \mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{online}}) $$
Furthermore, when $\lambda \to 0$, all three policies converge to the exact same value:
$$ \lim_{\lambda \to 0} \mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{oracle}}) = \lim_{\lambda \to 0} \mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{symptom}}) = \lim_{\lambda \to 0} \mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{online}}) = \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(p(m^*)) $$
where $m^*$ is the optimal static curriculum allocation from [[4. Curriculum Allocation.md]].

**Proof:**
1. Any policy feasible for the Online Assistant is a causal function of $(s^{(1)}, \dots, s^{(t)})$, which is a restricted information set relative to knowing $(s^{(1)}, \dots, s^{(T)})$ upfront. Hence $\mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{symptom}}) \le \mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{online}})$.
2. Knowing $(s^{(1)}, \dots, s^{(T)})$ is a sub-sigma-algebra of knowing $(x^{(1)}, \dots, x^{(T)})$, since $s^{(t)} = Bx^{(t)}$. By the properties of optimal stochastic control under coarser information filtrations, $\mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{oracle}}) \le \mathcal{L}(\pi^*_{\text{symptom}})$.
3. When $\lambda = 0$, the first term $\lambda \sum_{t=1}^T \ell_{\text{train}}$ vanishes identically. The objective depends strictly on $\mathcal{L}_{\text{grad}}(p(m(T)))$, which is a function solely of the total exposure count vector $m(T) \in \mathbb{N}^n$ satisfying $\sum_{j=1}^n m_j(T) \le cT$. Since any valid sequence $F_1, \dots, F_T$ generates a feasible integer partition $m(T)$, and the optimal allocation $m^*$ (the program of [[4. Curriculum Allocation.md]], with its budget read as $\sum_j m_j \le cT$, $m_j \le T$; that note writes $\sum_j m_j = T$, i.e. $c = 1$) is achievable by a fixed schedule in all three settings, all three objectives converge to the exact same minimal value. $\blacksquare$
