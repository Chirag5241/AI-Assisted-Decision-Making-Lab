In [[<G> - 6. Missing Assistance (Paused Learning)]] the human stops learning when the model leaves. Here they keep learning: on every round alone they pick their own features, see them, and those beliefs keep converging under the same curve $\psi$. We define the solo learning path and the expected decision regret along it, then plug it into the missing-model objective.

Notation as in [[<G> - 6. Missing Assistance (Paused Learning)]]: $L(m) = \kappa_d\big(1 - \rho(F^*(p(m)), p(m))\big)$, $F^*(p) = \arg\max_{|F| \le c} p_F^\top \Gamma_{FF} p_F$, ties broken by a fixed rule.

---

### 1. The solo learning map

On a round without the model, the human:
1. picks $F^*(p(m))$ — by [[2. Test Selection]] this depends on $p$ and $\Gamma$ only, **not on $s$**;
2. sees $x_{F^*}$, acts, and has expected regret $L(m)$ (by [[3. Graduation Regret]]);
3. learns from what they saw exactly as in training: $m_j \leftarrow m_j + 1$ for $j \in F^*(p(m))$, and $p_j$ follows the same curve $p_j(m_j) = (1-\psi(m_j)) d_j + \psi(m_j) p_j^{(0)}$.

Define the **solo map** on count vectors
$$ G(m) := m + e_{F^*(p(m))}. $$
$G$ is deterministic: because test selection ignores $s$, the human's whole solo path $m, G(m), G^2(m), \dots$ is fixed by $m$ alone. Features outside the salient set stay frozen; features inside it converge toward $d_j$ as $\psi$ dictates.

*(A slower solo learner can be modelled by giving solo exposures their own curve, $p_j = (1 - \psi(m_j + \eta\, m_j^{\text{solo}}))d_j + \dots$ with $\eta \in [0,1]$; $\eta = 0$ is paused learning. Everything below takes $\eta = 1$.)*

### 2. Expected decision regret along the solo path

An absence of $k$ rounds that starts at counts $m$ costs (discounting each round by $\delta$)
$$ \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \delta^i L\big(G^i(m)\big) $$
and returns the human at counts $G^k(m)$. If the model never returns, the solo value is
$$ W(m) := \sum_{i=0}^{\infty} \delta^i L\big(G^i(m)\big), \qquad 0 \le W(m) \le \frac{2\kappa_d}{1-\delta}. $$
Paused learning is $G = \mathrm{id}$, giving $W(m) = L(m)/(1-\delta)$.

**Missing model with continued learning.** In the setting of [[<G> - 6. Missing Assistance (Paused Learning)]] (model leaves at $\tau$ for good), the post-departure cost $\sum_{t > \tau}\delta^{t-\tau}\ell_{\text{grad}}$ now has expectation $\sum_{i \ge 1} \delta^i L(G^{i-1}(m_\tau)) = \delta W(m_\tau)$. Proposition 1 there becomes
$$ \mathcal{L}(\pi) = \sum_{t=1}^{\infty} q^{t-1}\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\lambda R(F_t \mid s^{(t)}; p(m_{t-1})) + (1-q)\,\delta\, W(m_t)\Big], $$
$$ J(m, s) = \min_{|F| \le c}\Big\{\lambda R(F \mid s; p(m)) + (1-q)\,\delta\, W(m + e_F) + q\,\bar{J}(m + e_F)\Big\}. $$
The proof is unchanged: only the value of the tail after $\tau$ changed.

**$L$ along the solo path need not decrease.** Learning only moves beliefs toward $d$, but it also changes *which* features the human looks at. Extinguishing a distraction can hand the top salience to a misunderstood feature, and $L$ jumps up (example in §3).

---

### 3. The independent case ($B = 0$, $\Sigma$ diagonal, $c = 1$)

Assume $\psi(m) \downarrow 0$. Use Lemma 1 of [[<G> - 6. Missing Assistance (Paused Learning)]]: with $\sigma_j := \Sigma_{jj} d_j^2$ and salience $S_j(m) := \Sigma_{jj}\, p_j(m_j)^2$, the human reads $j_m = \arg\max_j S_j(m)$ and
$$ L(m) = \kappa_d\Big(1 - \operatorname{sgn}(p_{j_m} d_{j_m})\sqrt{\sigma_{j_m}/d^\top\Sigma d}\Big). $$

**Fact (beliefs stay on a segment).** For $m' \ge m$, $p_j(m') - d_j = \frac{\psi(m')}{\psi(m)}\big(p_j(m) - d_j\big)$, so $p_j(m')$ lies between $p_j(m)$ and $d_j$. Hence $|p_j(m')| \le \max(|p_j(m)|, |d_j|)$, and if $p_j(m)d_j > 0$ then also $|p_j(m')| \ge \min(|p_j(m)|, |d_j|)$ with the sign kept.

**Proposition 2 (Distractions extinguish themselves).** If the human is reading a distraction ($d_{j_m} = 0$) and some other feature has $S_i(m) > 0$, then within finitely many solo rounds they stop reading it. While they read it, $L = \kappa_d$.

**Proof.** While $j := j_m$ is read, only $m_j$ grows and $S_j = \Sigma_{jj}\psi(m_j)^2 (p_j^{(0)})^2 \to 0$, while $S_i$ stays at $S_i(m) > 0$. $\rho = 0$ by Lemma 1. $\blacksquare$

**Proposition 3 (When a misunderstood feature corrects itself).** Let the human read $j$ with $p_j d_j < 0$, and let $m_j^{\text{flip}} := \min\{k : \psi(k) < \frac{|d_j|}{|p_j^{(0)}| + |d_j|}\}$ ([[1. 2 Actions Reduction]]). If
$$ \Sigma_{jj}\, p_j\big(m_j^{\text{flip}} - 1\big)^2 \;>\; \max_{i \ne j} S_i(m), $$
the human keeps reading $j$ until its sign is right; from then on $L \le \kappa_d(1 - \sqrt{\sigma_j/d^\top\Sigma d})$ for as long as $j$ stays most salient.

**Proof.** On the wrong-signed part of the segment $|p_j|$ decreases with $m_j$, so the smallest salience before the flip is at $m_j^{\text{flip}}-1$; the others are frozen meanwhile. $\blacksquare$

If the condition fails, $|p_j|$ passes near zero, the human switches to another feature, and $j$ is left frozen with the wrong sign (or exactly at $0$).

**Proposition 4 (Where the solo human ends up; blind spots).** Run the solo map forever from $m$. Let $\mathcal{S}_\infty$ be the features read infinitely often. Then:
1. every $j \in \mathcal{S}_\infty$ has $p_j \to d_j$, and all of them share the same informativeness $\sigma_j = \sigma^*$;
2. every other feature ends frozen with salience $\le \sigma^*$;
3. if $\sigma^* > 0$, the human's loss is eventually constant at $\kappa_d\big(1 - \sqrt{\sigma^*/d^\top\Sigma d}\big)$; if $\sigma^* = 0$, the human eventually reads only decaying distractions and $L = \kappa_d$ from then on.

**Proof.** $\mathcal{S}_\infty \ne \emptyset$ (infinitely many rounds, finitely many features), and $\psi \to 0$ gives (1)'s convergence, so $S_j \to \sigma_j$ for $j \in \mathcal{S}_\infty$. If $\sigma_i > \sigma_j$ for $i, j \in \mathcal{S}_\infty$, eventually $S_i > S_j$ always, so $j$ is never read again — contradiction. If a frozen feature had salience $> \sigma^*$, it would eventually beat every feature of $\mathcal{S}_\infty$ and be read — contradiction. For (3), once $p_j$ for $j \in \mathcal{S}_\infty$ has the sign of $d_j$, Lemma 1 gives the value. $\blacksquare$

**Blind spot.** A feature $k$ with $\sigma_k > \sigma^*$ is learned by nobody: the solo human never reads it again, because its frozen belief makes it look less important than it is ($\Sigma_{kk}\, p_k^2 \le \sigma^* < \Sigma_{kk}\, d_k^2$). In particular, solo learning reaches the best possible loss $L_{\min}$ iff $\sigma^* = \max_j \sigma_j$ (iff $j^* \in \mathcal{S}_\infty$ when $j^*$ is unique). **Continued learning repairs what the human looks at; it cannot discover what they do not look at.** Making such a feature salient is work only the model can do — see [[<G> - 8. Returning Assistance]].

**Example (non-monotone solo path).** $n = 2$, $\Sigma = I$, $d = (0, 1)$, $p^{(0)} = (3, -1.5)$, $\psi(m) = 1/(1+m)$. The human reads the distraction ($L = \kappa_d$), extinguishes it far enough that the misunderstood feature takes over ($L = 2\kappa_d$), whose first exposure shrinks it below the distraction again, and so on; per-round regret runs
$$ \kappa_d,\ \kappa_d,\ 2\kappa_d,\ \kappa_d \,(\times 10),\ 2\kappa_d,\ \kappa_d\,(\text{a few rounds}),\ 0,\ 0, \dots $$
(the second $2\kappa_d$ exposure flips $p_2$ to $+\tfrac16$; the distraction then decays below it) and the human ends perfectly calibrated with no help. With $p^{(0)} = (3, -1)$ instead, the first exposure puts $p_2$ exactly at $0$ ($\psi(1) = \tfrac12$); feature 2 is then a blind spot and the human reads the decaying distraction forever ($\sigma^* = 0$). (Both checked by direct simulation.)
